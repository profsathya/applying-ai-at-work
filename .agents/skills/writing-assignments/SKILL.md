# Writing Assignments

Follow [the authoring contract](../../../docs/AUTHORING.md). Use [writing-learning-goals](../writing-learning-goals/SKILL.md) for the capability and value, and [writing-to-teach](../writing-to-teach/SKILL.md) where explanations or examples are needed.

## Make the work executable

Check the following needs in the form appropriate to the supplied design. These are review questions, not mandatory headings or separate submissions.

| Need | Authoring decision |
|---|---|
| Purpose | What capability is this work developing, and why use it here? |
| Prior work | Which existing artifact, evidence, or skill should the participant bring? Where can they refresh it? |
| Task | What should they do first, and what sequence or choices lead to the intended result? |
| Success | What evidence satisfies the task, and what distinguishes a well-supported response? |
| Submission | What exactly is handed in, in what supported form, and where, according to approved metadata and directions? |
| Reflection | When useful to the design, what did the evidence change and what will the participant do with it next? |

Name prior artifacts specifically and check that they exist or are planned earlier. Do not assume that everyone has evidence which the course has not yet asked them to gather. If a prerequisite is missing, report the dependency and its effect; do not fabricate earlier work.

Write positive actions with an observable result. Give essential directions at the point of use. If a task calls for a judgment such as "credible evidence," explain the relevant distinction or link to a specific available explanation.

For example: "Compare the two delays in your process map. Choose one to investigate and cite a note from your stakeholder conversation that supports the choice." A response that names a delay without linking it to a note is incomplete evidence for that judgment. A polished AI-generated explanation without stakeholder evidence is not stronger merely because it sounds confident.

## Separate teaching, action, and self-check

Review the lesson, response prompt, and criteria as one participant experience. The lesson explains the idea and useful examples; the prompt requests the action and deliverable; criteria describe what successful evidence looks like. Do not restate the procedure in all three. A short reminder at the point of use is appropriate when it prevents searching, but criteria must not hide instructions needed to attempt the task.

Use short, descriptive task headings instead of putting a paragraph of directions in a heading. For guided assignments, keep save/copy/submission mechanics in the shared controls and include only task-specific exceptions in standing instructions. Preserve sufficient directions for independent work and the existing assessment contract.

Consolidate the directions for each response and place that response immediately after its teaching and action sequence using supported section references. Keep essential teaching visible; collapsible self-checks may hold evaluative guidance. Do not force multiple distinct responses into one box to imitate a compact example.

Use the existing presentation controls to make the primary completion action easy to find and secondary utilities unobtrusive. Avoid adding duplicate copy, save, or submission directions in the body. Keep optional status and points near submission when the renderer supports it. Read [the implementation reference](../../../docs/AUTHORING_PRESENTATION.md) before selecting compact presentation; its constraints do not apply to all assignment types.

## Align expectations across the artifact

Check that the example response, task, rubric or success criteria, submission type, and any AI activity prompts request compatible work. Do not ask for a spreadsheet in the body when the approved submission contract only permits a text response. Surface the conflict if resolving it would change the design or delivery contract.

Use the supplied grading policy, points, due dates, and rubric. Do not import an upstream late-work policy, invent scoring tiers, add arbitrary word counts, or create extra deliverables to fill a template. Clear ungraded practice can use simple success criteria without becoming an assignment.

If the intended difficulty is making a decision under uncertainty, preserve that difficulty while making the procedure and expectations clear. Ambiguous directions are not a substitute for meaningful uncertainty.

Where reflection serves the next activity, connect it to a decision: "Which assumption changed, and how will that affect the question you ask next?" Omit a separate reflection section when the requested artifact already captures that reasoning or the design excludes it.

Use [reviewing-course-text](../reviewing-course-text/SKILL.md) to check the finished artifact in sequence. A single-artifact worker returns findings to its parent rather than writing a second file.
