# Audience

The audience for this certificate is working professionals. Not students. This distinction drives every design decision.

## What "working professional" means concretely

They have jobs right now. They report to managers, serve customers, coordinate with colleagues, and navigate organizational politics. Their time is not their own — they are fitting this certificate around work responsibilities, family obligations, and everything else in an adult life.

They have stakeholders right now. Real people in their organizations who are affected by the problems they will work on in this course. They do not need simulated stakeholders or role-play exercises — they need course mechanics that connect to the people they already work with.

They have institutional knowledge the course should leverage. They know how their organization actually works — the formal processes and the informal ones, the stated priorities and the real ones. This knowledge is the substrate the course builds on. A participant who has spent five years in healthcare operations knows things about their organization that no case study can replicate.

Their time is constrained. Asynchronous and self-paced is not a convenience — it is a requirement. They may be doing coursework at 9pm after the kids are in bed, or during a quiet afternoon between meetings. The course must respect that reality in its pacing, expectations, and deliverable scope.

They will not tolerate simulated stakeholder role-play when they have real ones. If the course asks them to pretend a classmate is their VP of Operations, they will disengage. If it asks them to actually talk to their VP of Operations about a real problem, they will see the value.

They are evaluating "is this worth my time" continuously. Every sprint must deliver value they can feel. Not value promised for later — value now. If Sprint 2 feels like busywork, they will mentally check out regardless of how good Sprint 4 is.

They may or may not have technical backgrounds. Some participants will be software engineers. Others will be operations managers, HR professionals, project leads, or analysts. The course should not assume either a technical or non-technical starting point. AI direction capability is not a technical skill.

## How this audience differs from undergraduates

This audience definition is what most distinguishes this certificate from CST349 and CST395, which target undergraduate students. Undergraduates are building foundational knowledge and habits of mind. Working professionals are adding a specific capability — AI direction — to an existing base of organizational knowledge and professional judgment. The pedagogy, pacing, examples, and expectations all differ as a result.
