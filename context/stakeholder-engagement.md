# Stakeholder Engagement

Real stakeholders, not simulated. This is a load-bearing differentiator for the certificate.

Working professionals have access to actual people in their organizations — managers, colleagues, customers, cross-functional partners — who are affected by the problems they will work on. Course design should require them to talk to these real stakeholders, not role-play with AI or classmates.

## What this means in practice

**Participants identify real stakeholders for their chosen problem.** During orientation, part of choosing a problem is identifying who in the organization cares about it, is affected by it, or has authority over it. These are the people the participant will engage throughout the course.

**Course mechanics include actual stakeholder conversations.** Each sprint should include at least one real touchpoint with a stakeholder — an interview, a feedback session, a validation conversation, a demo. The specific format will vary by sprint and by participant context, but the requirement for real human interaction is constant.

**Reflection includes what participants learned from those conversations.** Not what they expected to learn, but what actually happened when they talked to a real person. Stakeholder conversations produce surprises — disagreements, new information, shifted priorities — and those surprises are where learning happens.

**AI may help prepare for or analyze stakeholder conversations, but does not replace them.** AI can help a participant draft interview questions, organize themes from stakeholder feedback, or prepare a presentation. These are legitimate uses of AI as a partner. But AI cannot replace the conversation itself. The human relationship, the trust, the ability to read the room — these are human contributions.

## Connection to the Integrative Solver meta-habit

The Integrative Solver meta-habit requires authentic human relatedness. It is the capability to synthesize across stakeholder perspectives, which means actually holding those perspectives — not reading about them, not having AI summarize them, but hearing them from real people and making sense of the tensions between them.

Stakeholder engagement is one of the primary mechanisms through which participants develop integrative solving capability. If the course allows simulated stakeholders as the default, it undermines this meta-habit at its foundation.
