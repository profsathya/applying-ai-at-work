# Frameworks

This file documents the CTI frameworks that inform course design. Working professionals do not need to learn these names. The frameworks shape course design and instructor language, not necessarily participant vocabulary.

The frameworks are organized in layers, from foundation upward.

## Layer 1 — The Foundation: Read, Write, Listen, Talk

These are not communication skills. They are the mechanisms of thought itself. Reading closely, writing to think, listening actively, and talking to test ideas are how a person processes the world and constructs understanding. Without these, no amount of AI partnership produces real value — the participant becomes a consumer of AI output rather than a director of it. Course design should treat these as the substrate everything else rests on, and create explicit moments where participants exercise each.

## Layer 2 — The Three Meta-Habits

The meta-habits are behavioral practices that shape how a person engages with thinking, learning, and AI. They are how a capable practitioner shows up to the work.

- **Slow Down.** Resist the speed temptation that AI creates. Make space for thinking before acting. Do not accept the first AI output without consideration. Speed without judgment produces volume, not value.
- **Know Yourself.** Develop awareness of your own assumptions, biases, gaps, and strengths. Know what you bring that AI does not. Know what you do not yet understand well enough to direct AI on.
- **Take the Lead.** Direct AI rather than be led by it. Take responsibility for outcomes, decisions, and the human consequences of your work. Do not outsource judgment.

These three habits are practiced, not learned once. Course design should create repeated opportunities for participants to exercise each.

## Layer 3 — The Three Capabilities: SDL, IS, AB

The capabilities are what a participant develops over time by practicing the meta-habits in real work.

- **Self-Directed Learner (SDL).** Identifies what they don't know, designs how to learn it, and uses AI as a learning partner — not a shortcut around learning.
- **Integrative Solver (IS).** Synthesizes across stakeholders, domains, and perspectives. Requires authentic relatedness with real people, not abstract systems thinking alone.
- **Adaptive Builder (AB).** Tests assumptions, builds prototypes, pivots on evidence, and iterates toward working solutions.

The capabilities depend on the meta-habits. A participant who cannot slow down cannot become a self-directed learner. A participant who does not know themselves cannot integrate across perspectives honestly. A participant who will not take the lead cannot adaptively build.

## Layer 4 — Supporting Frameworks

These are intellectual tools the team uses to design experiences and that shape participant work without necessarily being named to them.

- **Symbiotic Thinking** — treating AI as a cognitive partner, not a tool
- **Superagency and Human Value Proposition (HVP)** — what becomes possible through human + AI partnership, and what makes the human contribution worth amplifying. These two concepts always appear together; neither stands alone.
- **3Cs** — Context, Choices, Confirmation; framework for AI interaction
- **UMPIRE** — thinking framework for problem navigation
- **DIKW** — Data, Information, Knowledge, Wisdom; the human contribution lives at the upper layers
