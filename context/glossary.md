# Glossary

These are CTI internal terms used in course design and team communication. They may or may not appear in participant-facing materials. This glossary is for the team.

---

**Read / Write / Listen / Talk** — The foundational layer of the CTI framework. Not communication skills, but the mechanisms of thought: reading closely, writing to think, listening actively, and talking to test ideas. All other frameworks rest on these.

**Self-Directed Learner (SDL)** — A meta-habit. Identifies what they don't know, designs how to learn it, and uses AI as a learning partner rather than a shortcut around learning.

**Integrative Solver (IS)** — A meta-habit. Synthesizes across stakeholders, domains, and perspectives. Requires authentic relatedness with real people.

**Adaptive Builder (AB)** — A meta-habit. Tests assumptions, builds prototypes, pivots on evidence, and iterates toward working solutions.

**Meta-habits** — Collective term for SDL, IS, and AB. These are not skills to acquire but ways a capable practitioner operates in an AI-augmented environment. All three work together and rest on the Read/Write/Listen/Talk foundation.

**Symbiotic Thinking** — Treating AI as a cognitive partner, not a tool. The human and AI each contribute things the other cannot, and the result is greater than either alone.

**Superagency** — The expanded capability that becomes possible through human + AI partnership. Always paired with HVP.

**Human Value Proposition (HVP)** — What makes the human contribution worth amplifying: judgment, context, stakeholder relationships, organizational knowledge, accountability. Always paired with Superagency.

**3Cs (Context, Choices, Confirmation)** — Framework for AI interaction. Provide context so AI understands the situation, generate choices so the human has options, seek confirmation so decisions are deliberate.

**UMPIRE** — Thinking framework for problem navigation. A structured approach to moving through complex, ill-defined problems toward actionable framing.

**DIKW Pyramid** — Data, Information, Knowledge, Wisdom. Describes the hierarchy from raw data to actionable wisdom. The human contribution lives at the upper layers (Knowledge and Wisdom), where organizational context and professional judgment determine what the data means and what to do about it.

**Science of Computing** — CTI's term for the discipline. Deliberately not "Computer Science" and not "computing literacy."
