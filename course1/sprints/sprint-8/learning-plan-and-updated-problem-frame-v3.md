# Learning Plan and Updated Problem Frame

## Purpose

Plan how you will close the gaps in your Problem Frame, and update the frame so it shows what you know, what you are still assuming, and how you will find out.

You are not doing the learning now. You will follow this plan in CIS 502. What you write here needs to be clear enough that you could pick it up on the first day of that course and start.

Continue in Part B of your Learning Plan template, then Part C. Your Dojo Lab bullets are the raw material: pick from them, cut what did not survive, and write the five questions in full sentences, in your own words. Two things the Dojo never touched are yours to add here: the reason each gap matters to your frame, and the updated frame itself.

## Part B. Your learning plan

For each gap in your plan, in rank order, answer five questions. One or two sentences each is enough.

1. **What do I need to learn?** The gap, in one line, and whether it is blocking or useful.
2. **Why does my Problem Frame need it?** The part of the frame that depends on it, from A4.
3. **Where will I learn it?** The sources: a person, a document, a system, an observation. Name them. A person counts; say how you will reach them.
4. **What steps will I take, and in what order?** Small steps you could finish in one sitting, with a rough time for each.
5. **How will I know I learned it?** What you will be able to show, and the independent thing you will check it against, which could confirm it or prove you wrong.

*Example, gap 1:*

1. **Learn:** Whether the other account managers really find out late when an account changes hands. Blocking.
2. **Why:** Part 3 of my frame, who is affected, says all four managers are. If they are not, the problem is smaller than I said.
3. **Where:** The four account managers. Dana, who sees every handover. The last three handover emails.
4. **Steps:** First, ask Dana for the dates of the last three handovers (15 min). Then ask each manager when they found out about those handovers (four short chats, about 1 hour). Then compare the dates (30 min).
5. **Know:** I can say, for each of the last three handovers, how many days passed before each manager knew. Confirmed if most managers found out more than a day late; wrong if most knew the same day. Checked against: the managers' own answers, set beside the handover email dates.

If a gap does not fit in five small steps, it is probably a topic. Go back and narrow it.

## Part C. Your updated Problem Frame

Your frame from Sprint 3 is already in C1. Leave it there. Copy it into C2, "My updated frame," and in C2 update **parts 6 and 7** so they match your plan:

- **Part 6, assumptions.** Keep each assumption marked confirmed or inferred. For each one still inferred, add which gap in your plan will check it, in place of "who could tell me."
- **Part 7, what you do not know yet.** Rewrite it so it lists the gaps in your plan, in rank order, plus the interesting ones you left out.

If writing the plan showed you that another part of the frame is wrong or vague, fix it, and note which part you changed.

*Example, part 6:*

- **From part 3:** All four managers are affected. **Status:** inferred. **Checked by:** Gap 1 in my plan.
- **From part 4:** Nobody has tried to fix this before. **Status:** inferred. **Checked by:** Gap 2 in my plan.

## Assessed on Part B, your learning plan

- Every blocking gap from Name the Gap is in the plan, in rank order.
- Each gap answers all five questions.
- The "why" points at a specific part of the Problem Frame.
- Sources are specific: a named person, document, system, or observation.
- Steps are small, in order, with a rough time.
- Each "know" names what you will show and something independent that could prove you wrong.

## Assessed on Part C, your updated Problem Frame

- C1 is unchanged from week 7, and C2 holds all seven parts, with parts 6 and 7 rewritten.
- Every inferred assumption in part 6 names the gap that will check it.
- Part 7 lists the planned gaps in rank order, plus the interesting ones left out.
- Any other change is noted with the part it touched.
