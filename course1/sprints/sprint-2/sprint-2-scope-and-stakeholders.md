# Sprint 2: Scope and Stakeholders

You have a problem statement. Now you need to know where it ends.

A problem without a boundary is not a problem you can solve - it is a complaint. This sprint asks you to draw the line: what is inside the scope of your problem, and what is outside it? That distinction is harder than it sounds. Scope creep is real, and so is scope collapse, where you narrow so tightly that the problem stops mattering. Getting this right requires talking to people, not just thinking harder.

This sprint introduces a second stakeholder. In Sprint 1, you spoke with someone close to the problem. This week, you will seek out someone whose perspective differs - a person who is affected differently, sits further from the center of the issue, or has reasons to see the problem in a way that challenges your current framing. What you hear from them will not confirm what you already believe. That is the point.

By the end of this sprint, you will have a cleaner problem boundary and a richer picture of who actually has a stake in the outcome.
