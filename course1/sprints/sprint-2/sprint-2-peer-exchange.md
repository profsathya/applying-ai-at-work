# Sprint 2 Peer Exchange

You have spent this sprint narrowing scope, talking to two stakeholders, and working out what the tensions between them mean for your frame. This discussion closes the sprint by putting that work in contact with your peers' work.

The goal is not to summarize what you did. It is to name the two hardest things - one call you made, one problem you have not fully solved - and then help someone else see something in their frame that they may be too close to see.

## Your initial post

Post before reading others' responses. Two things:

**1. One scope call that was harder than you expected.**

Describe a boundary decision you made - something you included, excluded, or left deliberately ambiguous in your scope statement. Explain what made it hard. Was there a stakeholder who fell on the wrong side of the line? A consequence you wanted to address but could not justify including? A place where the problem kept expanding when you tried to contain it?

Be concrete. "It was hard to decide where to draw the line" is not the post. The post names the specific call and why it cost you something to make it.

**2. One stakeholder tension you are still working through.**

Name a tension between the perspectives you have gathered - something your two conversations surfaced that your current frame does not fully resolve. This is not a failure of the assignment. It is honest work. Most real problems have at least one unresolved tension at this stage.

Describe what the tension is and why you have not resolved it yet. Is it a factual question you cannot answer without more information? A political reality that affects what you can recommend? A disagreement about cause versus symptom that cuts to the core of how you have framed the problem?

## Responding to peers

Respond to at least two peers. Your response should do one specific thing: name a tension in their frame that they may not be seeing.

This is not feedback on their writing or encouragement about their process. Read their post and ask: what is this person assuming that they have not named? What does their scope call imply about priorities or power that they did not make explicit? Who is not in their stakeholder map but probably should be?

A useful response completes this sentence: "The tension I notice in your frame is..." and then explains what you see and why it matters. You do not need to resolve it for them. Naming it clearly is the contribution.

Your response should be three to six sentences - long enough to be useful, short enough to be read.

## Evaluation

Initial posts and peer responses will be evaluated together on two dimensions:

- Your initial post is specific enough that a peer who does not know your organization could understand the actual call you made and the actual tension you are carrying.
- Your peer responses name a real tension in the frame - not a general observation about complexity, but something specific to what your peer wrote.
