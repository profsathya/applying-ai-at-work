# Start Here: Direct AI Deliberately

## Why This Sprint Matters

AI can generate options quickly, but quick output is not the same as trustworthy direction. Ask it to "write a project update for my client" and you get a fluent message that could have come from anyone about anything. This sprint teaches you to give AI enough context to be useful, ask for choices rather than one answer, and identify what must be confirmed outside the model.

You will use AI as a thinking partner, but you remain responsible for the goal, standards, choices, and final meaning of the work. Start with your own thinking, use AI to expand and test it, then decide what to keep, reject, revise, or verify.

## A Quick Example: Same Task, Two Ways

Take a task you actually own: telling a client that a deliverable will be two weeks late.

Undirected ask:

- Prompt: "Write an email telling my client the project is delayed."
- Result: a generic, apologetic message with a made-up new date and a vague reason. It reads fine, uses a confident tone that hides what it does not know, and commits you to things you have not decided.

Directed ask:

- Context: the client, the real reason for the slip, what is already done, and the tone your working relationship can carry.
- Choices: ask for two or three ways to frame the delay, each with its tradeoff (lead with the new date, lead with the cause, or offer a partial delivery first).
- Confirmation: before sending, check the revised date with your team and confirm the partial-delivery option is real, not assumed.

Same task, minutes apart. The second version is slower by a few minutes and far more likely to be something you can stand behind. That gap is the skill this sprint builds.

## What You Will Do

1. Bring forward your Sprint 1 problem frame.
2. Use Context, Choices, and Confirmation to structure AI conversations.
3. Critique one AI output and push back on it.
4. Create an exploration log that shows iterations, assumptions, decisions, and evidence needs.

## Time Guidance

Plan for focused work across two weeks. Short concept reading, Dojo practice, peer exchange, and a final artifact should all serve the same real problem rather than becoming separate assignments.

## Working Standard

Specific and honest work is better than polished generic work. Show what you tried, what changed, what evidence you have, and what remains uncertain.
