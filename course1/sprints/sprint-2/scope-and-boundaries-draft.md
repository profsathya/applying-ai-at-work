# Scope and Boundaries Draft

You have a problem. Now write down exactly where it ends.

This assignment asks you to produce an explicit scope statement: what this work will address, what it will not address, and why those lines are drawn where they are. Vague scope is the most common reason good work stalls. A boundary you have not named is a boundary you will cross without noticing.

## What to submit

Write three sections:

**1. In scope**

List the specific parts of your problem that this semester's work will address. Each item should be concrete enough that someone else could look at a situation and tell you whether it belongs. Aim for 3-6 items. If you can only write generalities, you are not done scoping yet.

**2. Out of scope**

List the adjacent issues this work will not address. These are real problems - they may even be related - but they are not yours to solve right now. Naming them explicitly protects your focus and sets honest expectations with stakeholders. Aim for 3-5 items.

**3. Boundary reasoning**

For each major boundary decision, write one to three sentences explaining why that line sits where it does. The reasoning should be grounded in three factors:

- Your role: what you have standing to address
- Your authority: what you can actually change or influence
- Your timeline: what is realistic in 10 weeks

This is not a place for abstract justifications. "This is out of scope because it falls under a different department's charter and I have no decision rights there" is useful reasoning. "This is out of scope because it is too complex" is not.

## Using AI to brainstorm

You may use an AI tool to generate candidate items for your in-scope and out-of-scope lists. If you do, note briefly which items came from that process. The reasoning section must be your own work - it depends on context about your role, your organization, and your constraints that no AI has access to.

A useful test: if your reasoning could have been written by someone who has never met you and knows nothing about your organization, revise it.

## A note on tightness

Scope that feels uncomfortably narrow is usually about right for a 10-week project. If your in-scope list describes your entire job function, you have not scoped yet. If a thoughtful colleague read your scope statement and said "that is very focused," that is a good sign.

## Submission

Paste your three sections directly into the text entry field. You do not need headers or special formatting - clear, specific prose is the goal. Total length is typically 400-700 words.
