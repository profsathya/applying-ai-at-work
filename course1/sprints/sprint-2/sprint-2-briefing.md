# Sprint 2 Briefing

This sprint runs two weeks. By the end of it, you will have three things:

1. **An explicit scope statement** - what counts as part of your problem, and what does not. Written out. Specific enough that a colleague could tell you when you are inside it or outside it.
2. **Notes from a second stakeholder conversation** - with someone whose relationship to the problem is different from the person you talked to in Sprint 1.
3. **A synthesis** - what the two perspectives together imply for how you are framing the problem.

## What this sprint is not

This sprint is not asking you to reconcile your two stakeholders. It is asking you to hold both perspectives at once and notice what that reveals.

You talked to one stakeholder in Sprint 1. That person gave you a view of the problem from where they sit. The person you will talk to in Sprint 2 sits somewhere else - they have different authority, different exposure to consequences, or different functional context. Their view will not match the first one exactly. That is expected. That is the point.

Disagreement between stakeholders is not a problem to resolve. It is information about where the real boundaries of the problem are.

## Your three deliverables

**Scope statement.** Write down what is inside your problem and what is outside it. Be explicit: "This problem includes X. It does not include Y." Scope statements that say everything tend to mean nothing. A useful scope statement should be falsifiable - someone should be able to hand you an example and ask whether it belongs.

**Second stakeholder conversation notes.** One conversation with someone whose perspective you did not get in Sprint 1. This might be a decision-maker who controls resources, a front-line person who absorbs the consequences, or a cross-functional partner who touches the problem from a different direction. The goal is the same as Sprint 1: hear how they describe the problem in their own words.

**Synthesis.** Two to four sentences on what changes, if anything, about your framing when you hold both stakeholder perspectives side by side. Where do they agree? Where do they diverge? What does the divergence tell you about what you may have underweighted?

## Pacing

Week 4: Draft your scope statement. Use your Sprint 1 deliverables as the starting point. Ask yourself what the edges of your problem are - what falls just outside it, and why.

Week 5: Have your second stakeholder conversation. Write your synthesis afterward. Submit all three deliverables before the week ends.
