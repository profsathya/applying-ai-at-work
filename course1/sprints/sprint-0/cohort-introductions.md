# Cohort Introductions

Before you dive into your own problem this week, take 10 minutes to post an introduction and read what your cohort has written. You are sharing a professional space with people whose organizational contexts are likely very different from yours. That difference is useful. Their questions will surface assumptions you didn't know you had.

## Your post

Write a short introduction (200-300 words) that covers three things:

1. **Your role and organization.** Your title, the type of organization you work in (industry, size, sector - no need to name it if you'd rather not), and a one-sentence description of what you actually do day to day.

2. **Your professional context.** What problems land on your desk? Who do you serve, report to, or coordinate with? Give enough detail that your cohort can picture the pressures you're navigating.

3. **One AI situation from your workplace.** Describe one specific instance where AI has already shown up in your work - or where it is being pushed onto you. This does not need to be a success story. It could be a tool being rolled out, a process being automated, a decision being handed to an algorithm, or a capability you've started using on your own. Be specific: what was the situation, and what was your reaction to it?

Keep the tone professional and concrete. This is not a social icebreaker. You are mapping the terrain of the cohort so everyone can draw on the range of experience in the room.

## Your replies

Reply to **two** people whose professional context differs meaningfully from yours - different industry, different role type, or a different relationship to AI. Your replies should do one of the following:

- Point out a parallel you see between their situation and yours, and note where it breaks down.
- Ask a question that would help you understand their context better.
- Name an assumption you noticed in their framing and say what prompted it.

Avoid generic encouragement. The goal is to build a shared picture of how AI is actually landing across your cohort's organizations.

## Logistics

Post your introduction by the end of Day 3 of the week. Post your two replies by the end of Day 5. Discussions that happen only at the deadline tend to be one-sided. Getting in early gives your cohort something to respond to.
