# Ingredients and Settings

Bread machine success usually comes down to two things: accurate ingredients and a cycle that matches the dough. You do not need to memorize bread science. You do need to know what each ingredient is doing so your first troubleshooting step is practical instead of random.

## The basic ingredient roles

**Flour provides structure.** Bread flour usually gives a taller, chewier loaf because it has more protein. All-purpose flour can work for many basic loaves, but the loaf may be slightly softer or lower. Whole wheat flour absorbs more liquid and often needs a whole wheat cycle.

**Liquid hydrates the flour.** Water is the simplest choice. Milk adds tenderness and browning. Too much liquid can create a sunken or gummy loaf. Too little liquid can leave a dense loaf with dry flour in the corners.

**Yeast raises the dough.** Bread machine yeast or instant yeast is the most straightforward choice. Active dry yeast may work if the recipe is written for it. Old yeast is a common reason for a flat loaf.

**Salt controls flavor and yeast activity.** Do not skip it unless you are using a recipe designed for that change. Too little salt can let the dough rise too quickly and collapse.

**Sugar feeds yeast and supports browning.** A small amount helps the loaf. Too much sugar can over-brown or change the rise.

**Fat improves tenderness.** Oil or butter can make the crumb softer and extend freshness. Too much can weaken structure.

## Measure like you want to learn from the result

For a working professional fitting bread into a full week, repeatability matters. The fastest path is to reduce avoidable variation.

Use this measuring routine:

1. Put the pan on a stable surface before adding ingredients.
2. Measure liquid at eye level.
3. Spoon flour into the measuring cup and level it off if you are measuring by volume.
4. Weigh flour if the recipe gives grams.
5. Use separate measuring spoons for yeast and salt if possible.
6. Mark any changes you make directly in your notes.

Do not scoop flour straight from the bag with the measuring cup. It packs the flour down and can add more flour than the recipe expects.

## Choose the right cycle

Your machine labels may vary, but these settings are common.

**Basic or White.** Use for simple loaves based mostly on white flour. This is the best first cycle for most machines.

**Whole Wheat.** Use when the recipe contains a large share of whole wheat flour. It usually gives the dough more time to hydrate and rise.

**French.** Use for lean loaves with little or no fat and sugar. It often creates a thicker crust.

**Sweet.** Use for enriched doughs with more sugar, eggs, butter, or add-ins. Do not use it for your first loaf unless your recipe requires it.

**Dough.** Use when the machine mixes and rises the dough, but you shape and bake it elsewhere.

**Bake only.** Use for a separate bake stage. This is not the normal choice for a first basic loaf.

For your first loaf, choose the cycle named in the recipe. If the recipe does not name a cycle, use Basic or White for a simple white loaf.

## Match loaf size and crust setting

Loaf size controls timing and heat. A 1.5-pound recipe in a 2-pound setting may overbake. A 2-pound recipe in a 1-pound setting may crowd the pan or underbake.

Choose:

- The loaf size listed in the recipe.
- Medium crust unless you have a reason to choose light or dark.
- No delay timer for the first loaf.

Once you understand your machine, crust setting becomes a preference. On the first loaf, it is another variable to keep steady.

## Troubleshoot from evidence

Use the finished loaf and your dough note together.

| What you see | Likely cause | Next adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Dense, heavy loaf | Too much flour, old yeast, or not enough liquid | Check yeast freshness and add liquid carefully next time |
| Sunken top | Too much liquid, too much yeast, or not enough salt | Reduce liquid slightly or confirm salt measurement |
| Dry flour in corners | Not enough liquid or uneven mixing | Scrape corners early or add water 1 teaspoon at a time |
| Gummy crumb | Too much liquid or loaf sliced while hot | Cool fully before slicing and reduce liquid slightly |
| Pale crust | Light setting, low sugar, or recipe style | Use medium crust or confirm sugar amount |
| Very dark crust | Dark setting or too much sugar | Use medium or light crust and recheck sugar |

Troubleshooting is not about fixing every problem at once. Change one thing, bake again, and compare.
