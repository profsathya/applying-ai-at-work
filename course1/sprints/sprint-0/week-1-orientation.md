# Week 1: Orientation

This week is about one thing: choosing the problem you will work on for the next 10 weeks.

Not a case study. Not a hypothetical. A real problem from your real workplace, affecting real people, with real consequences.

You have spent years building organizational knowledge that no one outside your institution has. This course asks you to put that knowledge to work. By the end of Week 1, you will have committed to a specific problem and produced an initial map of the people most affected by it.

## What you will do this week

- Read the course overview and understand what is expected across the 10 weeks.
- Introduce yourself to your cohort, including a sketch of your professional context.
- Identify and commit to the workplace problem you will develop throughout the course.
- Map the stakeholders connected to that problem.

## How this week sets up everything that follows

The problem you choose here shapes all four sprints. Each sprint builds on the last, moving you from a first rough framing toward a sharp, stakeholder-validated problem statement. The stronger your starting choice, the more useful each sprint becomes.

Choose a problem that:

- Is specific enough that you can name the people affected by it.
- Is real enough that someone in your organization would care about the outcome.
- Is open enough that the framing is still genuinely uncertain.

Avoid problems where you already know the answer and are working backward. The value of this course comes from working through genuine ambiguity with structure and stakeholder input.

## By the end of Week 1

You will have submitted your problem commitment and an initial stakeholder map. These are not final - they will evolve. But they are public commitments, and the course is designed to hold you accountable to working with real people and real constraints.
