# Initial Stakeholder Map

Before you can work on a problem, you need to know who is in the room -- even the people who never show up to the meeting. This assignment asks you to make that visible.

Your map does not need to be a polished diagram. A photo of a whiteboard sketch, an annotated list in a spreadsheet, a rough diagram drawn in any tool you have handy -- all of these are acceptable. What matters is the thinking, not the format.

## What to submit

Produce a stakeholder map for the problem you chose in the previous assignment. For each stakeholder, capture three things:

1. **Role and relationship.** Who is this person relative to the problem? Name their actual role (not just "manager" -- "operations manager for the Pacific region" is better). Then state how they connect to the problem: do they cause it, feel its effects, have the power to change it, or something else?

2. **Type of stake.** For each stakeholder, mark which of these applies:
   - **Authority** -- they have formal decision-making power over this problem or its solution
   - **Influence** -- they shape what gets done, even without formal authority
   - **Impact** -- they are affected by the problem, whether or not they have any power over it

   A stakeholder can have more than one. Most real stakeholders do.

3. **First conversation candidate.** One person on your map is the right first conversation for Sprint 1. Flag them. In one or two sentences, say why you are choosing them: what do you expect to learn, and why are they the right person to start with?

## Format options

Any of the following works:

- A diagram (drawn, diagrammed in any tool, or sketched on paper and photographed)
- An annotated table or list in a document or spreadsheet
- A mind map or sticky-note layout, photographed or exported

Upload a single file. PDF, image files (JPG, PNG), and common document formats (Word, Google Docs exported as PDF) are all accepted.

## A few things to watch for

Do not use placeholder labels like "stakeholder A" or "the executive team." Every entry should be specific enough that you could point to a real person or a clearly defined group.

If you are unsure whether someone belongs on the map, include them. It is easier to remove a stakeholder in Sprint 1 after a conversation than to discover mid-sprint that you missed a key person.

Your map will not be graded on completeness. You are at the beginning of the problem, and your understanding will shift. What will be graded is whether you have distinguished roles and stakes clearly, and whether you have identified a credible starting point for your first conversation.

## Due date

Submit before the end of Week 1. Your instructor will review your map alongside your problem statement. If there are questions about your stakeholder landscape before Sprint 1, you will hear back before it opens.
