# Choose Your Problem

This is the first real decision in the course. Choose well, because you are committing to this problem for the next nine weeks.

Your problem statement does not need to be polished. You will refine the wording over the coming sprints. What you are committing to now is the topic: the specific workplace situation you will frame, test, and present. That commitment does not change.

## What to submit

Write a response of 200-350 words that covers all four of the following:

1. **The problem in one or two sentences.** State it plainly. What is wrong, stuck, or worth fixing? Avoid jargon that only insiders understand. Write it so that someone outside your organization could grasp what you're pointing at.

2. **Why it matters right now.** Not in theory -- in your organization, this quarter or this year. What is at stake if this problem goes unaddressed? Name the business, operational, or human consequence.

3. **Who is affected.** Name the roles, teams, or groups who feel this problem most directly. Be specific: "frontline staff in our regional offices" is more useful than "employees." Include anyone who is causing the problem, not just those experiencing it.

4. **Why this problem is worth 10 weeks of your attention.** You have limited time and many competing demands. What makes this problem the right one to work on right now?

## What qualifies as a valid problem

Your problem must be:

- **Real.** It exists in your organization today, not in a case study, a past job, or a hypothetical scenario.
- **Current.** It is active and live, not something that was resolved before you enrolled.
- **Yours to engage with.** You have access to the people affected. You will be talking to them.

Problems that are hypothetical, borrowed from another organization, or drawn from historical situations will be returned without a grade. Choose something you can actually work on.

## A note on scope

Problems that are too large ("our culture needs to change") or too narrow ("one report takes me 20 minutes to format") tend to stall in later sprints. Aim for something that involves two or more people, has identifiable consequences, and is small enough that you could imagine moving it within the span of a few months. If you are unsure, submit what you have. Your instructor will give you feedback before Sprint 1 begins.

## Due date

Submit before the end of Week 1. Your instructor will review your problem statement and confirm it is a valid starting point before Sprint 1 opens.
