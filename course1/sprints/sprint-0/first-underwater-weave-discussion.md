# First Underwater Weave Discussion

Use this discussion to compare first-session evidence. The goal is not to present a perfect basket. The goal is to notice how setup, water, material, and tension affect the result.

## Your initial post

Write 200-300 words about your first underwater basket weaving setup or first practice attempt. Include:

1. **Practice setting.** Name the controlled water space you used or plan to use. State the safety boundary you followed, such as shallow water, head above water, and no cutting tools underwater.
2. **Materials.** Describe the reed or weaving material, soak time, basket size, and pattern.
3. **Technique observation.** Explain what happened while weaving. Focus on one concrete moment, such as a reed twisting, a spoke leaning, a row gapping, or the base drifting.
4. **Drying observation.** If your basket dried, describe what changed. If it has not dried yet, state what you will check later.
5. **Next adjustment.** Name one change you would make next time and why.

If you were not able to weave during the module window, post your planned setup and identify the specific technique risk you will watch for first. Return later and add a reply to your own post with the result.

## Your replies

Reply to two peers. Useful replies include:

- A question that helps diagnose a tension, spacing, or material issue.
- A suggestion for improving setup without adding risk.
- A comparison to your own observation where the same symptom had a different possible cause.
- A reminder to change only one variable during the next attempt.

Avoid generic praise. Focus on evidence from the post and offer a practical next move.

## What to look for across posts

As you read the discussion, look for patterns:

- Did certain materials split more often?
- Did tighter weaves pull inward after drying?
- Did tub or basin setup make the work easier than a pool setup?
- Did cloudy water or loose fibers affect pattern visibility?
- Did participants who paused for inspection catch problems earlier?

Use the shared observations to improve your next basket plan.
