# How to Get Help With Your Problem Frame

## Use Help To Make The Frame Stronger

A problem frame is not something you have to perfect alone. It should get clearer when another person asks what you mean, what evidence you have, who is affected, and what may still be assumed.

Ask for help when your frame feels too broad, too polished, too personal to test, or too focused on a solution. Those are normal signs that the frame needs another round of evidence and judgment.

## What To Bring

Bring enough context that another person can respond to your actual situation, not a generic version of it.

- Your starting goal or surface request.
- One concrete example that shows the problem.
- Your current problem statement.
- The people or roles affected by the problem.
- One assumption you may be making.
- The question you most need help answering.

## Who To Ask

Start with someone who can help you see the frame more clearly.

- Ask a peer to listen for vague language, missing context, or hidden solutions.
- Ask someone close to the setting whether the problem sounds real and recognizable.
- Ask an instructor or facilitator when you are stuck on scope, evidence, or course expectations.
- Use AI to generate questions or alternatives, but do not treat the first AI response as confirmation.

## How To Ask

Use a focused request instead of asking, "Is this good?"

- "What part of this problem statement is still too broad?"
- "What would you need to know before you believed this was the real problem?"
- "Who else is affected that I may be leaving out?"
- "Where does this sound like a solution instead of a problem?"
- "What assumption should I test before I carry this forward?"

## What To Do With The Feedback

Do not try to include every suggestion. Look for patterns. If two people are confused by the same word, example, or missing stakeholder, revise that part first.

After you get help, write a short note for yourself:

1. What changed in my problem frame?
2. What evidence or example made the change necessary?
3. What still needs to be tested later?

Keep that note with your Sprint 1 work. You will need it when you explain how your thinking changed across the course.
