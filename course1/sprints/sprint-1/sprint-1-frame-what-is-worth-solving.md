# Sprint 1: Frame What Is Worth Solving

In this sprint, you move from a goal or surface request to a real problem that is specific enough to test. The first frame does not need to be final. It needs to be useful enough to carry into stakeholder and AI-assisted exploration.

This sprint is part of Reframing Problems with AI. You will keep using the same real current problem throughout the course. A workplace problem is ideal, but the problem can also come from a job search, role transition, volunteer role, community work, caregiving or family system, campus work, professional portfolio, small business, or another real setting where actual people are affected.

By the end of this sprint, you should be able to:

1. Distinguish a goal from a problem worth solving.
2. Use the abstraction ladder to move between examples, patterns, causes, purposes, and workable scope.
3. Use AI to expand and challenge your thinking without accepting the first output.
4. Write a problem frame that is real, current, connected to actual people, and ready to test.

## Sprint Rhythm

1. Start by naming a real goal and the people affected by it.
2. Read the concept page on goals, surface requests, and underlying problems.
3. Use the Dojo as Framer and Skeptic after you write your own first thoughts.
4. Exchange problem frames with peers and listen for what still sounds vague.
5. Submit your Goal Plan and Problem Reframing Document.

## Portfolio Thread

Carry forward the work from this sprint into your living course portfolio. Keep the artifacts, AI prompts, pushback notes, stakeholder evidence, and reflections that show how your thinking changed.

## Completion Criteria

You are done when you can explain the difference between your starting goal and your current problem statement, and you can name what evidence you need next.
