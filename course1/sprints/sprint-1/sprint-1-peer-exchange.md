## Sprint 1 Peer Exchange

You have spent this sprint building, auditing, and testing a problem framing. You wrote a first-pass statement, surfaced the assumptions inside it with AI, and then took the framing out of your head and into a real conversation with someone affected by the problem.

This discussion closes the sprint. Its purpose is not to get validation - it is to encounter a problem that is structurally similar to yours but embedded in an organization you do not know. That friction is the point.

## Your initial post

Write two things:

**1. One assumption your stakeholder conversation shifted.**

Identify an assumption you held before the conversation that changed - or got complicated - because of what your stakeholder said. Be specific. Name the assumption as you held it going in, and describe what shifted. "I thought X. My stakeholder said Y. Now I'm less certain about Z" is the structure you are going for.

**2. One question you are now stuck on.**

What do you not yet know that you need to know? This should be a genuine open question - not rhetorical, not answered by the work you have already done. It could be about the problem itself, your stakeholder map, what a solution would need to address, or something you are uncertain about in your own framing.

Keep your post under 300 words. Precision matters more than length here.

## Your replies

Respond to two peers whose organizational context is different from yours. Different industry, different function, different type of institution - look for someone working in territory you do not recognize immediately.

In each reply, engage with what they are actually stuck on. You are not there to solve their problem or reassure them that their framing is solid. You are there to think out loud with them from the outside.

Useful reply moves include:

- Naming what the question reminds you of from your own context - and where the parallel breaks down.
- Asking a follow-up question that opens the stuck question further rather than closing it.
- Pointing to a tension or constraint in their situation that might not be visible from inside their organization.

Avoid replies that confirm, validate, or compliment the framing. Your peer does not need to hear that they are on the right track. They need someone outside their context to think with.

Each reply should be at least 75 words and address the specific question or assumption your peer raised.

## Evaluation

Your initial post earns credit for naming a real, specific shift - not a general observation about the conversation - and for surfacing an open question that is genuinely unresolved.

Your replies earn credit for engaging with the peer's actual situation rather than offering generic encouragement. The standard is: did your reply give the other person something to work with?
