# Sprint 1 Briefing

This sprint runs two weeks. By the end of it, you will have three things:

1. **A first-pass problem statement** - written, specific, yours. Not polished. Not final. A stake in the ground.
2. **An honest audit of your assumptions** - what you currently believe about the problem and why, including what you might be wrong about.
3. **Notes from one real conversation** - with a stakeholder who has skin in the game.

## What this sprint is not

This sprint is not asking you to get the problem statement right. It is asking you to surface what you actually believe about the problem so you can test it.

Most professionals carry a working theory about a problem long before they write it down. That theory has assumptions baked in - about who is affected, what is causing it, what a good outcome looks like. Some of those assumptions are solid. Some are not. You cannot know which until you name them.

Writing a first draft makes the theory visible. Auditing your assumptions makes it testable. Talking to a stakeholder starts the test.

## Your three deliverables

**Problem statement (first draft).** A few sentences, not a page. Name the problem, name who is affected, name what is at stake if nothing changes.

**Assumption audit.** Three to five beliefs your problem statement depends on. For each one, write down what would have to be true for the assumption to hold, and one thing that might contradict it.

**Stakeholder conversation notes.** One conversation. The goal is not to validate your framing - it is to hear how this person describes the problem in their own words. Note where your framing matches theirs and where it doesn't.

## Pacing

Week 2: Write your problem statement and assumption audit. Use AI if it helps you think - but write the final version yourself. What you know about your organization is not in any model.

Week 3: Have the conversation. Take notes. Submit your three deliverables before the week ends.
