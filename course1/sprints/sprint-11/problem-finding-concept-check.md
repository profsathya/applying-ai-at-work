  purpose: Check how you distinguish indicators, current states, goals, candidate
    gaps, and uncertain explanations before you examine and commit to a problem.
  builds_on: Start with what bugs you, Two other places to look, and your Candidate
    List. These questions use the ideas from that work.
  standing_instruction: Choose an answer for each question. Use Check my answer for
    formative feedback, revisit the explanation, and revise your choice if needed.
    Copy your final answers into the Canvas assignment and submit there. The five
    points are for completion reviewed by your instructor; the answer checks do not
    calculate or submit a grade.
  tasks:
  - id: recognize-a-candidate-gap
    kind: choice
    prompt: Compare these four statements about account handovers. Which one goes
      furthest toward a candidate problem rather than an indicator, a proposed solution,
      or a broad goal?
    options:
    - Nobody tells me when a client account changes hands.
    - Handovers happen between two account owners without an announcement. The rest
      of the team could know within a day, but instead people act on stale ownership
      and clients repeat information.
    - We need an app that announces every account handover to the whole team.
    - We want clients to have a smooth experience when their account changes hands.
    correct_index: 1
    explanation: The second statement describes the current state, a possible state,
      and the cost of the gap to identifiable people. The complaint is a useful indicator,
      the app is a proposed solution, and a smooth experience is a goal. You can investigate
      the candidate without committing to an app.
    criteria:
    - Distinguish an indicator, a gap, a proposed solution, and a desired outcome.
    - Look for a current state, a possible state, and a specific cost to people.
  - id: describe-how-it-works-now
    kind: choice
    prompt: 'The volunteer example starts with “New volunteers sign up, come twice,
      and stop.” Which follow-up best makes the next move: describing how it actually
      works now?'
    options:
    - Volunteers probably stop because they do not feel that their contribution matters.
    - The organization should give every new volunteer a mentor for their first month.
    - The volunteer experience should be more welcoming and consistent across shifts.
    - Signups go to a central inbox. On a first shift, the person running that day
      improvises something for the new volunteer to do.
    correct_index: 3
    explanation: Describe the observable sequence and who does what before diagnosing
      or proposing a fix. The central inbox and improvised first shift give you a
      current state to examine. You can then make further passes to find what the
      arrangement costs people.
    criteria:
    - Look for observable steps and who does what.
    - Distinguish current handling from a diagnosis, a desired state, or a proposed
      fix.
  - id: trace-a-surface-request
    kind: choice
    prompt: In the new-hire example, “We need an app to track where new hires are”
      leads to the goal that new hires should contribute to their teams as quickly
      as possible. What does reaching that goal statement allow you to do next?
    options:
    - Look for different problems standing in the way of that goal, then choose which
      one to investigate instead of treating the requested app as the answer.
    - Use the goal as evidence that the requested tracking app is the right project,
      then define the app’s features.
    - Replace the app request with a broader onboarding system so that every plausible
      cause is covered at once.
    - Use the goal itself as the final problem statement, since everyone would support
      it.
    correct_index: 0
    explanation: 'The goal opens up several candidate problems: handoffs, assignment
      expectations, or training that does not prepare people for the work. It does
      not decide which candidate you should pursue or which solution would help. That
      choice still requires your judgment about the actual situation.'
    criteria:
    - Keep the goal separate from both candidate problems and solutions.
    - Consider what the goal reveals and what it cannot decide for you.
  - id: keep-several-candidates-live
    kind: choice
    prompt: You have one candidate that feels promising and two rough indicators you
      have not examined. What is the strongest next step for the Candidate List submission?
    options:
    - Commit to the promising candidate now, then use the other two only if it later
      becomes impossible.
    - Write three variations of the promising candidate so that your submission has
      the required number of entries.
    - Work underneath the two rough indicators and write three candidate gaps before
      defending a favorite.
    - Ask AI to rank the promising candidate against two plausible problems typical
      of your role.
    correct_index: 2
    explanation: The source asks for three to five candidate problems so you have
      a real choice rather than a defense of the first idea. Work from your own context,
      investigate the rough indicators, and postpone commitment until you have alternatives
      worth comparing. Merely duplicating an entry does not create that choice.
    criteria:
    - Consider whether the next step creates meaningful alternatives from your own
      situation.
    - Keep gathering and describing separate from defending a choice.
  - id: show-what-you-do-not-know
    kind: choice
    prompt: In the handover example, “It has not been fixed because each individual
      handover feels small enough to handle informally” is a proposed explanation.
      If you have not checked that explanation, how should you include it in your
      Candidate List?
    options:
    - Keep it as a factual sentence because the explanation fits the process you described.
    - Keep it as your best current guess and put a question mark at the end, so the
      uncertainty remains visible.
    - Remove the entire candidate because you cannot yet explain why the gap has persisted.
    - Replace it with a more confident explanation before submitting, so the five-line
      gap statement is complete.
    correct_index: 1
    explanation: The fifth field asks for your best current guess about why the gap
      persists. The source asks you to mark uncertain sentences rather than sound
      more confident. Keeping the guess visible gives later inquiry something specific
      to test; uncertainty does not disqualify the candidate.
    criteria:
    - Distinguish a plausible explanation from an established fact.
    - Keep the five-field gap complete without hiding what you do not know.
