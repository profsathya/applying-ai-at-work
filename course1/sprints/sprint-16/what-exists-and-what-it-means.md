# What exists, and what it means

The write-up of the last two activities, in five short answers. Most of it is in your table and your boxes already. What is new here is the judgment in the last two questions, and that is where most of the points are.

## 1. What did you think already existed, before you asked?

Describe your first draft of your table in a sentence or two. Was it full? Did you have lots of questions or gaps? You submitted it in What solutions already exist, if you want to look back at it.

## 2. Through your conversation with your Dojo, how did your table change, and what did you make of it?

What changed on your table as a result of your conversation with the Dojo? Anything in column 1, existing solutions? How did it help you learn about those existing solutions? If nothing changed, say why you decided not to go with AI's suggestions. This is the second C, **Choices**, made visible: what you kept from what AI gave you, and what you did not.

> **Example.** Column 1 gained one row from AI, account transition plans, the kind agencies use; I kept it in the table but it will not survive here, two accounts a month cannot carry that much process. It did not change my other rows, but it told me the CRM field can notify a team when the owner changes, which I did not know, and it pointed me at the help page. I ignored "a name for this kind of problem"; it helped me search and told me nothing about my situation.

## 3. What did you look at, and what did you learn?

Which items on the table did you spend the most time looking at closely? Where did you look to find more information, and what did you find?

## 4. Does any of it change your problem?

There are at least three honest answers, and you may land between them. Nothing out there fits, so the problem stands as framed. Something fits, and getting it adopted here is the problem. Something fits and would simply solve it, so this may not be worth eight weeks, in which case your runner-up comes into view.

> **Example.** Something fits and adopting it is the problem. The owner field exists and nobody uses it. My problem was never that no mechanism exists; it is that nobody is made to use the one we have. Part 4a needs to say so.

## 5. What does this change about how you would describe your problem?

One paragraph. If the answer is "nothing," say what you looked at that could have changed it and did not.
