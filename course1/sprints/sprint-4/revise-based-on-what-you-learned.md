# Revise Based on What You Learned

You have now had the conversation. You heard how a decision-maker actually reads your problem frame - what landed, what did not, and where their understanding of the situation differs from yours. This assignment is where that conversation produces something concrete.

Revise your integrated problem frame based on what you heard. Then write a change log that documents what changed, what stayed, and why.

This revised frame is what you will bring to the capstone in Sprint 5. It is worth getting right.

## What to submit

Your submission has two parts, written directly in the text entry box.

---

### Part 1: Revised integrated problem frame

Write your full revised frame. Include all five elements: problem statement, stakeholder perspectives, scope, success criteria, and human and AI contribution split.

This is not a list of changes. It is the frame itself, revised and complete. A reader who has not seen your earlier drafts should be able to read this as a self-contained document.

If parts of your frame did not change, include them as-is. The goal is one coherent document that reflects your current best thinking.

---

### Part 2: Change log

For each section of the frame, note whether it changed and why. The format below works well, but any clear format is acceptable as long as the reasoning is visible.

**For each change:**

- What the original framing said
- What specific feedback or observation prompted the revision
- What the revised framing says and why that is a better frame

**For each section that stayed the same:**

- What the original framing said
- What you heard in the conversation that bears on this section
- Why you kept the framing as-is, including any feedback you considered but decided not to act on

Not every section will change. That is expected. A section that survived a decision-maker's scrutiny unchanged is worth documenting as such - but you need to show the reasoning, not just assert that the framing held up.

---

## On "nothing changed"

If your conversation produced no revisions, that is a defensible outcome - provided you can explain it. A frame that held up under real questioning from someone with authority over this problem is genuinely strong. The test is whether your change log shows your thinking or just reports the result.

A change log that says "the decision-maker agreed with my framing, so nothing changed" is not sufficient. One that says "the decision-maker questioned my scope boundaries; I considered narrowing to X but concluded Y because Z" shows that you engaged with the feedback seriously, even when the conclusion was to hold the line.

If you kept something because the decision-maker agreed with it, that is worth noting. If you kept something despite disagreement, that requires a more explicit defense.

---

## How this will be evaluated

- **Traceable revisions (10 points):** Changes in the revised frame connect directly to specific feedback from the validation conversation. Edits that sharpen language without altering the substance of the frame are fine, but they do not substitute for substantive revisions that respond to what you heard. The evaluator should be able to read your change log and understand exactly what changed and what drove each change.
- **Reasoning in the change log (10 points):** The change log explains the thinking behind every substantive change and every substantive non-change. "The decision-maker said my scope was too broad and I agreed" is a conclusion. "The decision-maker pointed out that two of my stakeholder groups operate under different reporting lines, which means scope decisions that work for one group create compliance issues for the other. I revised the scope to address one group in this frame and noted the second group as a dependency" is reasoning.
- **Revised frame is tighter (5 points):** The revised document is more precise and more defensible than your integrated problem frame draft. This may mean fewer words, sharper scope boundaries, cleaner success criteria, or a problem statement that no longer requires explanation. A frame that has been tested against a real decision-maker should show it.

---

## A note on this being your capstone artifact

The Sprint 5 capstone asks you to demonstrate this frame - to walk through your problem, your process, and your conclusions in a way that reflects your actual thinking and your organization's actual situation.

The revised frame you submit here is what you will demonstrate. Revisions you make after this submission are not prohibited, but this assignment is designed to give you a fully tested, defended frame before you go into the capstone. Use that.
