# Sprint 4 Briefing

This is the final sprint before the capstone. By the end of it, you will have one thing: a complete, validated problem frame that you can present to anyone in your organization - a colleague, a senior leader, a stranger to your team - and they will understand what problem you are framing, why it matters, and for whom.

## What you are building

Sprint 4 is about integration and validation. You have been building toward this for eight weeks. Now you pull the pieces together.

Your integrated problem frame combines:

- **Your problem statement** - the precise, scoped description of the problem you developed across Sprints 1 and 2
- **Your stakeholder perspectives** - who is affected, what they need, and what you learned from talking to them
- **Your success criteria** - the specific, testable conditions that would tell you the problem is solved
- **Your human-and-AI contribution breakdown** - what AI can do in this situation and what only you can bring

These are not separate documents stapled together. They form a single, coherent argument: this is the problem, this is why it matters, this is who it affects, this is what solved looks like, and this is how you would approach it.

## The test this sprint applies

Imagine handing your document to someone who does not know your organization. Can they follow it? Do they understand the problem without you explaining it? Would they agree the success criteria actually measure whether the problem is solved?

That test is not hypothetical. You will take your frame to a decision-maker - someone with genuine authority over whether this problem gets prioritized and worked on - and you will find out whether it holds up. Not someone who will nod politely. Someone who will tell you what is missing, what is unclear, or what does not match how they see the situation.

The revision you make after that conversation is part of the deliverable. What you hear from a decision-maker, and what you change as a result, is evidence that your framing is grounded in organizational reality, not just your own perspective.

## Pacing

**Week 8:** Write the integrated frame. Start with what you already have from Sprints 1-3. Your job this week is not to invent new content - it is to find where the pieces do not connect and fix those gaps. Look for places where your problem statement and your success criteria do not quite line up, or where your stakeholder notes contradict your scope. Those tensions are the work.

**Week 9:** Take the integrated frame to a decision-maker. Give them enough context to evaluate it honestly, then ask them where it is wrong or incomplete. Revise based on what you hear. The conversation and the revision are both part of the sprint submission.

## What distinguishes strong frames from weak ones

A weak integrated frame reads like a collection of answers to different questions. Each section is fine, but they do not build on each other.

A strong frame reads like a case. The problem statement sets up the scope, the stakeholder section explains who is affected and why it matters to them specifically, the success criteria follow directly from that - and the contribution breakdown names the human judgment that has been present throughout. You can see the thinking.

Your goal this week is to get to the second kind. Read your draft as if you are the decision-maker. Where would you push back? Fix those places before the conversation.
