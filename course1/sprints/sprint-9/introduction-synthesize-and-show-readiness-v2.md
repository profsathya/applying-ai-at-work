# Introduction: Synthesize and Show Readiness

For four sprints, you have been building one problem. In Sprint 1 you framed it. In Sprint 2 you tested your assumptions and looked at what already exists. In Sprint 3 you heard from a real person. In Sprint 4 you named what you still don't know and planned how to find out.

This sprint adds no new material. It is where you put that work together into one document someone else can follow, and show that your problem is ready to carry into CIS 502. Being ready doesn't mean having every answer. It means knowing which ones you still need, and how you'll find them.

AI is your thinking partner again. In the Dojo Lab it listens to you explain your problem, reads your document like a stranger, and asks the questions someone who could help along the way might ask. You decide what to change. The argument, and the call on whether you're ready, are yours.

By the end of this sprint you will have two documents. Your Integrated Problem Document says what your problem is, what you know and how sure you are, how it changed, and what a solution would have to do. Your Readiness Report says whether your problem is ready to carry forward, and how you have grown in how you work with AI and with people. You got there the same way you have all term: your own thinking first, real people's views, and AI to test what you built.

## What you need for this sprint

- Your **Problem Frame document**, the one with "Last updated: Sprint 4" at the top.
- Your **Sprint 2 tables**: Dig into your assumptions and What solutions already exist.
- Your **Sprint 3 Stakeholder Map** and **Stakeholder Validation Report**.
- Your **Sprint 4 Name the Gap** and **Learning Plan** files.
- Your **four sprint reflections**.

## Sprint 5 Road Map

1. **Build your Integrated Problem Document** from the work you already have. Nothing new to research.
2. **Present it in the Dojo Lab.** Explain your problem, have AI read your document as a stranger, and answer the questions someone who could help along the way might ask. Then make your own fixes.
3. **Write your Readiness Report.** Show that your problem is ready to carry forward and what you still need to find out, say how you have grown, and name your next step.
4. **Reread your reflections.** Then write a short final one, without AI.

## Sprint 5 Concepts

**Write for someone who could help along the way.** Picture handing your document to a person who could help with your problem at some point: a supervisor, a team lead, someone in your community, or whoever fits. They haven't seen your work, and they don't know what a Problem Frame is or what you tested. Use plain words and keep solutions out. Could they read it once and explain your problem back to you?

- Hard to follow: "Part 2 of my frame is still inferred."
- Easy to follow: "When an account changes hands, nothing reaches the rest of the team."

**A requirement, not a solution.** In CIS 502 you will build toward a solution. Here, you name what any solution would have to do, without choosing one yet. A requirement comes from your Problem Frame or from what people told you. If it already names a tool or a fix, it is a solution, not a requirement.

- Already a solution: "We need a shared handover tracker."
- A requirement: "Everyone on the team knows within a day when an account changes hands."

**Confirmed and inferred.** The same marks you have used since Sprint 3. **Confirmed** means you checked it against something independent, and it held. **Inferred** means you believe it but have not checked it yet. By now, most of what you know is still inferred. That's expected. Your reader trusts you more when you say which is which.

- Overstated: "Clients are frustrated by every handover."
- Honest: "I've watched two handovers, and Dana described them the same way. I haven't heard from a client yet."

**Your verdict.** At the start and end of your Readiness Report, you make a call on your problem: ready to carry forward, ready with conditions, or not ready yet. Most of you will land on ready, or ready with conditions. What matters is the reason behind your call, and what you still need to find out.

## If you still have open questions

Putting it all together may show you that some of your evidence is thinner than you hoped. That's normal, and it's part of being ready. Name what's still open in your Readiness Report, and say how you'll find out. If your problem truly isn't ready to carry forward, say so, with your reason and your next step.
