# Puck Prep, Diagnosis, and Tasting

Espresso is a small drink with little room for hidden mistakes. Puck prep gives water an even path through the coffee. Diagnosis tells you what to change after tasting. Tasting notes help you separate preference from evidence.

## Puck prep sequence

Use the same sequence each time.

1. Dry and warm the portafilter basket.
2. Weigh the dose.
3. Grind into the basket or dosing cup.
4. Break up clumps and distribute grounds evenly.
5. Settle the coffee bed with a light tap if needed.
6. Tamp level and firm. Consistency matters more than maximum force.
7. Brush loose grounds from the rim and ears of the portafilter.
8. Lock in and start brewing promptly.

Uneven grounds or a tilted tamp can create channels. Water will take the easiest path, over-extract part of the puck, and under-extract the rest. The result can taste sharp and bitter at the same time.

## Extraction diagnosis

Use taste, time, and flow together. No single signal tells the whole story.

What you notice | Likely issue | First adjustment
---|---|---
Shot reaches target yield in 15 to 20 seconds and tastes sour, salty, or thin | Under-extraction from fast flow | Grind finer
Shot takes 35 to 45 seconds and tastes dry, harsh, or ashy | Over-extraction from slow flow | Grind coarser
Shot sprays, spurts, or blondes unevenly and tastes sour and bitter | Channeling or uneven puck prep | Improve distribution and level tamp before changing recipe
Shot tastes weak even though timing is close | Yield may be too high for the coffee | Stop the shot earlier
Shot tastes intense but muddy | Yield may be too low or grind may be too fine | Try a slightly longer yield or slightly coarser grind

When a shot tastes both sour and bitter, do not assume the recipe is impossible. Check puck prep first. Mixed defects often come from uneven extraction.

## Adjust yield for taste

Once grind and puck prep are stable, use yield to shape flavor.

- Shorter yield can increase body and concentration.
- Longer yield can increase clarity and sweetness, up to a point.
- Too short can taste sharp, salty, or heavy.
- Too long can taste hollow, dry, or bitter.

Keep the dose fixed while you explore yield. For an 18 g dose, compare 32 g, 36 g, and 40 g yields using the same grind setting if the flow allows it.

## Tasting notes that help diagnosis

Useful tasting notes are specific enough to guide the next shot. "Good" and "bad" are not enough.

Use this quick tasting structure:

Category | What to notice
---|---
Aroma | Sweet, nutty, floral, roasty, smoky, stale
Acidity | Bright, sharp, pleasant, sour, flat
Sweetness | Caramel, chocolate, fruit, low sweetness
Bitterness | Balanced, dry, harsh, burnt
Body | Thin, silky, heavy, syrupy
Finish | Clean, lingering, dry, metallic, hollow

Write notes immediately after tasting. Espresso changes quickly as it cools, and memory fills gaps with guesses.

## Practical target

A practical target is a shot you can repeat, explain, and improve. The final cup does not need to match a cafe competition standard. It should taste intentional, and your notes should show why you made each adjustment.
