# Pull and Document an Espresso Shot

Pull a series of espresso shots, diagnose what happened, and submit the recipe you would repeat. This assignment is about practical control. You are not expected to produce a cafe-perfect shot on the first attempt.

## What to do

Use an espresso machine and grinder you can access. Keep the same coffee throughout the assignment.

1. Choose a starting recipe. A good baseline is 18.0 g dose, 36.0 g yield, and 25 to 30 seconds.
2. Prepare the puck with a consistent sequence.
3. Pull the shot and record the result.
4. Taste the shot and write notes immediately.
5. Make one adjustment.
6. Repeat until you have at least three attempts.
7. Choose the shot you would repeat and explain why.

If your equipment uses a different basket size, adjust the dose to fit the basket and keep the same logic. For example, a 16 g basket might start near a 32 g yield.

## What to submit

Submit one text entry with the following sections:

**1. Final repeatable recipe**

List dose, yield, timing method, final time, grinder setting, and puck prep sequence.

**2. Shot log**

Include at least three attempts. The log can be a Markdown table or a concise list.

**3. Diagnosis**

Choose one less successful shot and explain what likely happened. Use evidence from dose, yield, time, flow, puck prep, and taste.

**4. Tasting notes for the final cup**

Use at least four categories: aroma, acidity, sweetness, bitterness, body, and finish.

**5. Next improvement**

Name one change you would test next and explain why. Keep it to one variable.

## Evaluation

Your submission will be evaluated on the quality of your process, not on whether the final cup is flawless. Strong work shows that you can measure the recipe, prepare the puck consistently, diagnose extraction, and make a reasoned next adjustment.
