# Espresso Workflow Setup

A strong espresso workflow is practical, repeatable, and measurable. The goal is not to chase a secret recipe. The goal is to make a small set of controlled decisions, taste the result, and adjust one variable at a time.

A good cup usually has sweetness, clarity, balanced acidity, enough bitterness to give structure, and a finish that does not feel harsh or hollow. Crema can be useful feedback, but taste and consistency matter more.

## Start with a simple recipe

Use one clear starting point before changing anything.

Item | Starting target
---|---
Dose | 18.0 g ground coffee in the basket
Yield | 36.0 g espresso in the cup
Ratio | 1:2 coffee in to espresso out
Time | 25 to 30 seconds
Timing method | Start timing when the pump starts, then use that method every time

This recipe will not be perfect for every coffee or machine. It gives you a clean baseline. Once you know what the baseline tastes like, you can make informed adjustments.

## Set up the grinder

Espresso depends on a grinder that can make small, repeatable changes. A good grinder setting creates enough resistance that water extracts flavor evenly instead of rushing through the puck.

Use these grinder habits:

- Warm up the machine and portafilter before brewing.
- Weigh whole beans before grinding, or weigh the grounds after grinding.
- Change grind size in small steps.
- Purge a small amount of coffee after each grind change if your grinder retains grounds.
- Keep dose and yield steady while you dial in grind.

Finer grind usually slows the shot and increases extraction. Coarser grind usually speeds the shot and decreases extraction. That rule is the center of espresso diagnosis.

## Dose, yield, and timing

Think of dose, yield, and timing as the recipe controls.

**Dose** is the weight of dry ground coffee in the portafilter. Use the basket size as your guide. An 18 g basket usually works well near 18 g. Overfilling can create messy prep and uneven flow. Underfilling can leave too much headspace and reduce resistance.

**Yield** is the weight of espresso in the cup. A common starting yield is about twice the dose. With an 18 g dose, stop near 36 g out.

**Timing** helps you notice whether the grind and puck prep are in range. A 1:2 shot in 25 to 30 seconds is a useful starting target. Time alone does not define quality. A 28-second shot can still taste bad if the puck channeled or the coffee is not suited to that recipe.

## Work from a shot log

Professionals do not rely on memory when small changes matter. Use a simple shot log.

Shot | Dose | Yield | Time | Grind setting | Taste | Next adjustment
---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|---
1 | 18.0 g | 36.0 g | 18 sec | 6 | Sour, thin | Finer grind
2 | 18.0 g | 36.0 g | 27 sec | 5 | Brighter, sweeter | Keep setting

The log makes your decisions visible. If the shot improves, you know what helped. If it gets worse, you know what to reverse.

## One variable at a time

Do not change grind, dose, yield, and puck prep all at once. That makes the next shot hard to interpret.

Use this order for most dial-in work:

1. Choose the dose and target yield.
2. Adjust grind until timing is close.
3. Improve puck prep if flow is uneven or the flavor is both sour and bitter.
4. Adjust yield for taste after the shot is flowing consistently.

This workflow turns espresso from guessing into practical diagnosis.
