# Human Judgment Articulation

Your capstone demonstration shows your stakeholders and peers what you built. This submission shows something different: what you brought to the build that AI could not.

This is the most important thing you submit this week. It is also the shortest. One to two pages. The quality standard is not length -- it is specificity.

## What to write

Answer the four prompts below. Write directly. A few sentences per prompt is enough if those sentences are concrete. Vague claims ("I used my professional judgment throughout") earn no credit. Specific ones do.

1. **What organizational context shaped your problem frame that AI did not know?** Name it. This might be institutional history, a failed initiative, an informal power dynamic, a constraint that does not appear in any document, or knowledge about how your organization actually makes decisions versus how it says it does. What did you carry into this process that AI had no access to?

2. **Which stakeholder relationships influenced what you included or excluded from your frame?** Identify at least one specific relationship -- a person or group -- and explain how knowing them changed the scope, the framing, or the success criteria. "I know this team will reject anything that implies additional headcount, so I framed the problem around process efficiency rather than staffing" is the level of specificity this prompt is looking for.

3. **Which prioritization calls could have gone another way?** At some point you made a decision about what mattered enough to be in scope and what did not. You chose this problem over other candidates. You drew the boundary here rather than there. Name at least one of those calls and explain what you were weighing. There is no correct answer -- the point is that the decision was yours, not AI's, and you can account for it.

4. **Where did you stop and reconsider what AI produced?** Describe at least one specific moment when you looked at what AI generated -- a framing, a stakeholder list, a question, a draft -- and decided it was wrong, incomplete, or pointed in the wrong direction. What did you see that AI could not? What did you do differently as a result?

## What this is not

This is not a reflection on the course. It is not a summary of what you learned. It is not a celebration of the process.

It is a professional accounting of the judgment you exercised. Write it the way you would write a note to a colleague who needs to understand why the frame looks the way it does -- because you made specific calls that shaped it, and those calls need to be documented.

## Submission format

Type your response directly into the text entry box. You do not need headers or a formal structure, but the four prompts above should all be addressed. Aim for one to two pages. Anything under 250 words is likely not specific enough. Anything over 600 words is likely repeating itself.
