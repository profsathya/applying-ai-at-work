# Week 10: Capstone

You have spent nine weeks building toward this. You chose a real problem, scoped it to something specific, mapped the people it affects, tested your assumptions against actual conversations, and revised your frame in response to what you heard. This week, you present what you built.

The capstone is not a final exam. It is a demonstration. You will show your validated problem frame to peers and, where possible, to a stakeholder in your organization. The work is already done. This week makes it visible.

## What you will do this week

- Present your complete, validated problem frame to your peer cohort.
- Articulate the human judgment behind the frame: the decisions you made, the assumptions you revised, and what you contributed that AI could not.
- Give structured feedback on two peers' presentations.

## What a strong capstone looks like

A strong capstone presentation does not just describe a problem. It shows evidence of a thinking process. Your peers and stakeholders should be able to see how your frame evolved - where you started, what challenged it, and what you changed as a result.

The most important part of your presentation is not the problem statement itself. It is the account of what you brought to the process: the organizational knowledge, the stakeholder relationships, the judgment calls that shaped the frame. That is what only you could have provided.

## By the end of Week 10

You will have presented your problem frame and submitted a final reflection documenting what changed across the course and what you can now do that you could not do in Week 1.
