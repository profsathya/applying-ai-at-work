# Capstone Demonstration

This is the deliverable your stakeholders see. Across the sprints, you built a validated problem frame: you scoped it, mapped the people it affects, tested your assumptions in real conversations, and revised your thinking based on what you heard. This demonstration makes that work visible to an audience outside this course.

## What to produce

Create a demonstration of your validated problem frame. Format is your choice:

- A recorded walk-through: video, audio with slides, or screen capture with narration
- A narrated slide deck: slides plus speaker notes substantial enough to stand alone without a live presenter
- A polished written artifact: a structured document that walks an outside reader through the full frame

There is no preferred format. Choose the one that fits how you communicate in your professional context and how your stakeholders consume information. Someone whose stakeholders read documents should produce a document. Someone whose colleagues prefer a five-minute video should produce a video. The format choice is itself a professional judgment.

## What the demonstration must cover

Regardless of format, your demonstration should address all of the following:

1. **The problem.** A clear statement of the problem you chose, scoped to something specific and actionable.
2. **Stakeholders.** Who is affected, who has decision authority, and how they relate to the problem.
3. **Scope and success criteria.** What a resolved version of this problem looks like, and what is explicitly out of scope.
4. **Human and AI contributions.** Where AI supported your work and what you brought to the process that AI could not: organizational knowledge, stakeholder relationships, judgment calls about what mattered.
5. **How the frame evolved.** Where you started versus where you ended. Show the first-pass framing and the final validated frame side by side, or walk through the specific moments when your thinking changed.

The most important thing you can show is the thinking process, not just the conclusion. An outside audience should be able to see that this frame was tested, not just asserted.

## Sharing with a stakeholder

Share your demonstration with at least one person from your stakeholder map before you submit. This does not require a live meeting. A recorded video they can watch when available, or a document they can read, is enough.

After sharing, collect their response. Include it as part of your upload. The response can be:

- A brief written reply (email, message, or typed summary you draft based on a conversation)
- A short recorded reaction (audio or video)
- A written summary of what they said if they responded verbally

The stakeholder response does not need to be a formal endorsement. It can be a question they raised, a correction they offered, or a note on how they see the problem differently. What matters is that you closed the loop.

## What to upload

Upload a single submission that includes:

1. Your demonstration (the file or a link to a hosted recording)
2. The stakeholder response (inline, as a separate file, or as an addendum at the end of the demonstration document)

If you are uploading a link to a recorded video, paste it into a document file and upload the document. Do not submit a bare URL as the only file.

## How this is evaluated

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Demonstration clearly conveys the full validated frame to an outside audience | 20 |
| Evolution of the frame across sprints is visible (first-pass vs. final) | 10 |
| At least one stakeholder's response to the final frame is included | 10 |
| Format choice suits your context and is professionally executed | 10 |

**Total: 50 points**

A strong submission shows a frame that has been shaped by real input from real people, and makes that shaping visible. A weak submission presents only a final conclusion without showing what tested it or what changed.
