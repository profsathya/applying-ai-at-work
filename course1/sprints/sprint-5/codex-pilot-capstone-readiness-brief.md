# Codex Pilot: Capstone Readiness Brief

Before you submit the capstone, pause for one final quality check. This brief is meant to help you inspect the work you already have, not restart it.

Your capstone should show a validated problem frame and the thinking behind it. A reader should be able to understand what problem you chose, why it matters, who it affects, what evidence shaped the frame, and where your judgment changed the work.

## Readiness questions

Use these questions to review your current capstone draft, outline, or notes.

1. **Is the problem specific enough for action?** A strong frame names the workplace problem clearly and avoids drifting into a broad concern that no one can act on.
2. **Can a stakeholder see themselves in the work?** The final artifact should make clear who is affected, who has influence, and what each group needs to understand.
3. **Is the evidence visible?** Your reader should see how real conversations, observations, or workplace materials tested your assumptions.
4. **Is your judgment visible?** Name the choices you made: what you kept, what you excluded, what you changed, and where you redirected AI output because it missed something important.
5. **Is the next step credible?** The final recommendation or direction should fit the constraints of your workplace, not just sound reasonable in general.

## How to use this brief

Choose the two questions above that expose the biggest risk in your capstone. Spend your revision time there first. A focused improvement to the weakest part of the work is usually more valuable than polishing every section equally.

If you used AI to help draft, summarize, organize, or critique your work, make sure the final submission still shows your contribution. The capstone is not only about the output. It is about the professional judgment behind the output.
