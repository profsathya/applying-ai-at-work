# Hiragana, Katakana, and Romaji Practice

Japanese uses kana to represent sounds. Hiragana is often used for native Japanese words and grammar. Katakana is often used for borrowed words, names, emphasis, and sound-based spelling. Romaji writes Japanese sounds with the Roman alphabet.

For this module, use kana and romaji together. Kana helps you practice the writing system. Romaji helps everyone read your introduction.

## Reading tips

  * Read kana by sound. Each kana usually represents one sound unit.
  * `し` is usually written as `shi`, not `si`.
  * `ち` is usually written as `chi`, not `ti`.
  * `つ` is usually written as `tsu`, not `tu`.
  * `ふ` is usually written as `fu`, not `hu`.
  * In `わたしは`, the `は` is pronounced `wa` because it marks the topic.
  * Long vowel sounds in katakana often use `ー`, as in `テイラー` for Taylor.



## Practice steps

  1. Read the model introduction from the first page out loud using the romaji.
  2. Match each kana you recognize to the chart.
  3. Write your name in katakana, or choose the closest katakana spelling you can find.
  4. Draft three or four lines of introduction in Japanese.
  5. Add romaji pronunciation under each line.
