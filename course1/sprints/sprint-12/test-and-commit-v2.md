# Test and commit

Bring your cumulative table and finish the **Concept check** before starting. Use your own observations, without AI, to choose a problem worth investigating. Rough writing is enough; your instructor reviews your candidate gaps, check answers, uncertainty, and reasons.

## Part 1. Choose rows to examine

Select **three to five rows** with a described current state and an emerging gap. If more than five qualify, take the clearest gaps. Keep set-aside rows in your original table.

Fewer than three? Revisit **Things you stopped noticing** and **Things somebody handed you**, then watch your routine for a couple of days. Look for recurring tasks you brace for, repeated explanations, or workarounds. If every row concerns only you, look for one involving other people you could learn from later.

Record your selections and set-aside rows. If you still have fewer than three, explain where you looked and what remains missing.

## Part 2. Describe each candidate as a gap

Give each candidate **four lines**, as below. Use your table for the title and current state; describe a possible better state and who pays the cost. Quantify costs when you can support the estimate. Mark uncertain claims with **(?)**.

> **Illustrative worked example · Report formatting**
>
> **Title:** Analysts repeat the same report-formatting work.
>
> **Right now:** Export columns do not match our monthly report. Each analyst fixes a separate copy by hand.
>
> **It could:** One corrected copy could serve everyone using the report. (?)
>
> **The gap costs:** Fifteen analysts spending thirty minutes each would mean 7.5 hours monthly. I know my own time; I need to check the others' time. (?)

## Part 3. Check every candidate

Answer from what you know now. You do not need interviews or proof of causes yet. A clear **“cannot answer yet”** identifies what you need to investigate.

### 1. Real people

Name the people or roles paying the cost and where you can find them. You can be one of them. “Four account managers handling renewals in my team” is specific; “everyone in the company” is not.

### 2. Current handling

Complete **“X handles this today, but it does not ___.”** Name a system, process, habit, or person and the precise shortfall. Look for someone coping manually before concluding that nothing handles it.

“The outgoing owner tells the new owner, but the message does not reach colleagues” identifies a shortfall. “HR handles it, but not very well” leaves the gap unexplained.

### 3. Live now

Identify something you could observe or fresh evidence you could see **within two weeks**. A past or hypothetical example needs a current instance you can reach; otherwise, set it aside.

### 4. Workable size

Describe the current state in one paragraph, name someone other than you who would talk with you, and identify something you need to find out. You may refer to your Part 2 description if it fits in one paragraph.

The other person needs a stake in the situation or relevant knowledge. For a household problem, choose someone **outside the household** whom it would be normal to ask, such as a pharmacist or care coordinator for caregiving logistics.

**Adjust the size if needed:** if one decision resolves it and there is nothing to find out, look for the larger pattern. If you cannot describe the current state or identify specific people, narrow it to one instance you have seen.

### Record your verdicts

For each candidate, record its title, all four answers, and these verdicts:

- **Checks 1–3:** passes / does not pass / cannot answer yet.
- **Check 4:** right size / too small, go up / too big, go down; or state what you cannot answer. Record any scope change.

Set clear failures aside. For murky answers, record what you would need to find out. Make sure each verdict follows from your answer, rather than an assumption.

## Part 4. Commit or identify your next search

Choose the most workable candidate that matters to you. State it as a gap, then explain:

- **Why this one:** connect your choice to the checks.
- **Why not the others:** give one or two sentences per alternative, including those that mostly passed.
- **Why you:** describe your connection and why you want to stay with it.
- **Runner-up:** name your fallback, or say none is currently workable.

> **Illustrative comparison**
>
> Handovers and volunteer onboarding passed the first three checks. I chose handovers because I work on them weekly and can reach people who know the process. My only volunteer contact is hard to reach, so onboarding is my runner-up if I find a better route to learn about it.

If nothing survives, or the only survivor does not matter to you, revisit wrong-size candidates and set-aside rows, then the two later practice activities. Report what you tried and your next search step instead of forcing a commitment. Keep uncertainty marked.

Keep your full table and final answers for the framing work that follows.
