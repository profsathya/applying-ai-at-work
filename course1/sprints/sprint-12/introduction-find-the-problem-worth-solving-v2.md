# Introduction: Find the Problem Worth Solving

Choose a problem you can investigate through this course. Begin with your own experience; you do not need a problem picked out yet.

A **problem** is a gap between how something works now and how it could work, where the gap costs someone something real. A **candidate problem** is one you are considering.

![Three colleagues review a workflow and pass a folder across a worktable.](assets/workplace-handoff.webp)

*Work moves between people. Illustration created with AI for this course.*

## Your first-half route

> **From observations to a choice**
>
> 1. **Gather**
>
>     Build one table across **Start your list and get underneath it**, **Things you stopped noticing**, and **Things somebody handed you**.
>
> 2. **Check your understanding**
>
>     Use the six-question **Concept check** to test the distinctions you have practiced and review the feedback.
>
> 3. **Choose**
>
>     In **Test and commit**, compare three to five candidates and explain your choice, alternatives, and uncertainty.

Spread this work across several days in Week 1. **Things you stopped noticing** asks you to observe your routine for a day or two, so keep your table where you can add to it.

## What to keep and submit

- **Three practice activities · optional · 0 points.** Submit to record completion, or continue without submitting.
- **Concept check · 5 points.** Your instructor reviews completion.
- **Test and commit · 35 points.** One submission: candidate descriptions, four checks, choice with reasons, and runner-up.

Work **without AI** in this half. Use rough, specific notes; mark uncertainty with **(?)** and report honestly when a search produces nothing. You are choosing something to investigate, not proving its cause.

Keep your full table, including set-aside rows, and your Test and commit answers. You may need an alternative if your thinking changes.
