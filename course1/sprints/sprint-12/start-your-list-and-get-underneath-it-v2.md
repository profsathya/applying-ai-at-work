# Start your list and get underneath it

## 1. Start your list

Take five minutes. Write down three or four things that bug you.

Use your own experience at work or elsewhere, and try to include something involving other people. A phrase per item is enough. Work without AI.

## 2. Look underneath it

An **indicator** is a sign that something may be wrong. Describe how it works now to find the gap underneath it.

The example below follows an observation to a possible gap.

> **Worked example · Volunteer onboarding**
>
> 1. **Notice**
>
>     New volunteers come twice, then stop.
>
> 2. **Describe the process**
>
>     The shift lead gives each arrival whatever task is easiest to hand off.
>
> 3. **Find the possible gap**
>
>     Nobody plans the first month. Volunteers may lack an ongoing role, leaving regulars to cover the work. (?)

The follow-up question is **“Who plans a volunteer's first month?”** It reveals a part of the process that the first description left out.

Describe actions, rather than blame (“nobody cares”) or fixes (“buy an app”).

## 3. Build your table

Use these three columns:

- **What bugs you:** one item from your list.
- **How it actually works now:** two or three sentences about who does what, and in what order.
- **The gap starting to show:** one sentence about what could be different and who feels the cost.

Fill in each row. Take one further by asking **“How does that part work now?”** two or three times where useful. Stop when the gap and the people affected are specific. Add the detail to your table and mark uncertain claims with **(?)**.

Stuck? Describe the last time it happened. If nothing emerges, leave that part blank and mark the row for **Things somebody handed you**. If you have no starting observations, say so; the next activities offer other places to look.

Keep your table to build on in the next activity.
