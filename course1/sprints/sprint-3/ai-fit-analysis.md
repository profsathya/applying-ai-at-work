# AI-Fit Analysis

This is the first deliverable for Sprint 3. Before you design any solution, answer the foundational question: does AI actually belong here?

This is not a rhetorical exercise. Your answer might be yes, no, or "only for part of this." Any of those answers is correct if it is honest and grounded in your problem.

## What to write

Write a structured analysis of 400-700 words covering the four areas below.

**1. What would AI actually do?**

Name the specific tasks or functions AI would perform in a solution to your problem. Be precise. "Use AI to improve the process" is not an answer. Answers look like: "classify incoming requests by urgency," "draft initial summaries from long documents," "surface patterns across six months of incident reports," "generate options for a staff member to choose from."

If you cannot name specific tasks, that is a signal: you may not yet have a clear enough problem to assess fit.

**2. Signals that AI fits**

Identify characteristics of your problem that make AI a reasonable option. Consider:

- Volume: Is there more data, text, or cases than a person can reasonably process?
- Breadth: Does a useful solution require integrating information across sources that do not currently talk to each other?
- Pattern recognition: Would spotting patterns across many similar cases add value, and do you have the data to support it?
- Consistency: Is there a task where variation in human output creates problems, and where more consistent output would help?

Name the ones that apply. Ignore the ones that do not.

**3. Signals that AI does not fit**

Identify characteristics of your problem that make AI a poor fit or a liability. Consider:

- Relationship and trust: Does solving this problem depend on someone feeling heard, respected, or understood by a person - not a system?
- One-off judgment: Is this a rare, high-context decision where the right answer depends on details that do not repeat across cases?
- Accountability: Does the decision or output need a human name attached to it for legal, ethical, or organizational reasons?
- Adoption risk: Would introducing AI into this process create resistance that makes the actual problem worse?

Be honest here. A strong analysis takes both sides seriously.

**4. Your conclusion**

State your position: AI fits, AI does not fit, or AI fits for a defined subset of the problem. Explain why in two to four sentences. Your conclusion should follow from what you wrote in sections 2 and 3. It should also be grounded in your specific organization - not in what AI can do in general.

If your conclusion is that AI does not fit, submit that. It is the right answer if it is the true answer. Participants who reach this conclusion have done the analytical work correctly.

## What to use

Pull from your problem statement, scope statement, and stakeholder notes from Sprints 1 and 2. Your analysis should be specific to your problem, not generic. A reader who does not know your organization should be able to tell, from your submission, that you are reasoning about a real situation.
