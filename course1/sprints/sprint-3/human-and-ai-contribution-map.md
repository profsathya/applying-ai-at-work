# Human and AI Contribution Map

Your AI-fit analysis named where AI belongs in your solution. Your success criteria survived a real stakeholder conversation. Now do the harder thing: map exactly who contributes what, and hold yourself to specifics.

This assignment produces a two-column map. The left column is AI's contribution to your problem. The right column is what you and your stakeholders must contribute. Each entry should be specific enough that someone unfamiliar with your organization could read it and understand what is actually happening.

## Why this matters

The common failure in human-AI partnership is treating AI as a faster version of yourself. That framing leads to designs where the human and AI are doing the same kind of work, just at different speeds. A clearer design separates the contributions by type: AI does things humans genuinely cannot do well at scale, and humans do things AI genuinely cannot provide at all.

Your map makes that separation visible and testable. If both columns contain the same kind of work, the map is telling you something important about your design.

## What to submit

Write your map as text entry using the structure below.

---

### Part 1: The contribution map

Build a two-column table or a paired list. Label the columns clearly.

**Column 1 - What AI contributes**

List each specific AI contribution to your problem. Name the task or function, not the category. Examples of the level of specificity required:

- Not: "AI processes data quickly"
- Yes: "AI scans three years of service request logs to surface patterns in which request types take longest to resolve"

- Not: "AI helps with communication"
- Yes: "AI drafts initial responses to common intake questions, which the coordinator reviews before sending"

You should have 3 to 6 entries. Each one should connect directly to the success criteria you finalized in your stakeholder conversation.

**Column 2 - What humans must contribute**

List each contribution that you or your stakeholders must make - things that are structurally outside what AI can provide for this problem in your organization. Be specific about who contributes what.

Three kinds of human contribution belong here, and your map should include all three:

- **Judgment:** Decisions that require reading organizational context, history, or politics. Name the specific decision and who makes it.
- **Relationships:** Actions that require trust, credibility, or standing with particular people. Name the specific relationship and what it makes possible.
- **Accountability:** Who owns the outcome if the work succeeds or fails. Name the person or role and what they are accountable for.

Domain knowledge alone is not enough for this column. "I understand the policy" is background, not a contribution. The contribution is what you do with that knowledge that AI cannot.

---

### Part 2: Evidence from your own work

Point to one specific moment from your work in this course so far where you contributed something AI could not have provided.

This is not a hypothetical. You have had stakeholder conversations, developed a problem statement, worked through your AI-fit analysis, and tested success criteria with a real person. In one of those moments, something happened that required you specifically: your relationship with that stakeholder, your read of the room, your judgment about what mattered, your decision to hold a position or change it.

Describe that moment in 3 to 5 sentences:

- What was the situation?
- What did you do or decide?
- Why could AI not have done this, even with full context?

Then connect it to your map: which entry in the human column does this moment support?

---

### Part 3: One honest observation

In one paragraph, identify the place in your map where the boundary between AI and human contribution is least clear to you. What is ambiguous? What would you need to know to resolve it?

This is not a weakness in your design. Every real problem has a gray zone. Naming it honestly is more useful than pretending the map is complete.

---

## A note on generics

Entries like "AI provides insights," "AI is unbiased," or "humans bring creativity" will not score well. Every working professional applying AI to any problem can write those entries. Your map should only be true for your problem, your organization, and your stakeholders. A reader should not be able to lift your entries and paste them into someone else's map without rewriting them.

## Submission

Submit as text entry. Use the three-part structure above. There is no required word count, but a focused submission that covers all three parts typically runs 400 to 600 words. Due by end of Week 7.
