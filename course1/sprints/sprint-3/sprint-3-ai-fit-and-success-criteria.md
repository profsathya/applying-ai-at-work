# Sprint 3: AI Fit and Success Criteria

You have a scoped problem and two stakeholder perspectives. Now comes the question that separates rigorous problem framing from wishful thinking: does AI actually belong here?

Not every workplace problem is better with AI in the solution. Some are. Some are not. Some would be made worse. Answering that question honestly requires you to look at your specific problem, in your specific organizational context, with the specific people who are affected. No case study can do that for you.

This sprint runs two weeks. By the end of it, you will have three things:

1. **An AI fit assessment** - a clear, reasoned position on whether AI meaningfully improves the solution to your problem, and why.
2. **A success criteria statement** - specific, observable conditions that would tell you the problem is actually solved. Not goals. Criteria.
3. **Stakeholder validation** - a short conversation with one stakeholder confirming that your success criteria match what they would actually accept as success.

## What this sprint is not

This sprint is not asking you to commit to building an AI solution. It is asking you to test a hypothesis.

You have been framing a problem. You are now asking whether AI is a genuine fit for that problem or a convenient assumption. Many professionals reach this point and realize their problem is fundamentally a process, communication, or prioritization issue - and that adding AI would not fix it. That is a valuable finding. It is not a failure. A well-reasoned conclusion that AI does not fit is exactly as useful as a well-reasoned conclusion that it does.

The success criteria piece is equally important. Most workplace problems stay unsolved not because solutions are unavailable, but because no one agreed in advance on what "solved" would look like. You are building that agreement now, before you go further.

## Why stakeholders define success, not you

You know the problem better than most. Your stakeholders know what they will accept as a solution. These are different things, and both matter.

Your success criteria statement is a draft until a stakeholder confirms it. Their job in the conversation is not to approve your framing - it is to tell you what they would need to see to believe the problem was resolved. Listen for the gap between what you wrote and what they say. That gap is your most important signal this sprint.

## Pacing

Week 6: Work through your AI fit assessment. Write down the specific reasons AI is or is not well-suited to your problem. Be explicit about what kind of task AI would need to do and whether that matches what AI is actually good at. Draft your success criteria statement.

Week 7: Have your stakeholder validation conversation. Revise your success criteria based on what you hear. Submit your AI fit assessment, success criteria statement, and a brief note on what changed after the conversation.
