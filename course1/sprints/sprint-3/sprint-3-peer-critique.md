# Sprint 3 Peer Critique

This discussion closes the sprint. You have done the analysis. Now pressure-test two of your peers' conclusions before the sprint ends.

## Your initial post

In a single paragraph, post two things:

1. **Your AI-fit conclusion in one sentence.** Not a summary of your analysis - the conclusion. "AI fits for this part of my problem because..." or "AI does not fit here because..." If you cannot state it in one sentence, your conclusion is not yet sharp enough.
2. **Your sharpest success criterion.** The one that, if met, would most clearly indicate the work succeeded. It should be specific enough that someone who does not work at your organization could tell you whether it was met.

Post by midweek so your peers have time to respond before the sprint closes.

## Your replies

Reply to two peers. Your job is not to affirm their thinking. Your job is to push back on it.

For each reply, address at least one of the following:

**On the AI-fit conclusion:**
- Is the conclusion actually supported by what they described, or does it amount to "AI seems useful here"? If you cannot tell why AI fits based on what they wrote, say so.
- Is there a signal they may have underweighted? A relationship-and-trust consideration, an accountability question, an adoption risk they mentioned but did not factor into their conclusion?

**On the success criterion:**
- Could a stakeholder at their organization read this criterion and say definitively "yes, that happened" or "no, that did not"? If not, what is still vague?
- Is this criterion actually within their control, or does it depend on factors they cannot influence?

A useful reply is two to four sentences that identify a specific gap and name it directly. "This looks solid" is not a useful reply. Neither is a list of generic questions. Find the one place where the reasoning is weakest and put pressure there.
