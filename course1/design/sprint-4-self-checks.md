# Sprint 4 Self-checks, v0.1

Two items, 50 points each. Passing: 35 points (70%).
Status: both parts in the guide's record shape, 2 October 2026. Documentation only.

In Sprint 4 learners make the plan and refine their Problem Frame. The learning itself happens in CIS 502.

Record shape and rules from `course1/design/self-check-guide.md`. Five lines per item, the guide's target. Each line names its part by letter and by header. Only the full-points lines go on the page; the rungs, ranks, tags, gate, and notes stay in this record.

### Name the Gap Canvas Walkthrough, 50 points

Total: 50. Passing: 35. Partial on every line gives 35.

Gate: an empty A2, What I do not know yet, is not a pass, whatever the other lines score. The gap list is what the Dojo Lab and the Learning Plan are built on.

Grading note. Line 2 scores once across all the gaps, including any under "More gaps": full when every gap is marked and every blocking gap names what is waiting on it, 3 when one gap misses, 1 when two or more do. For a useful or interesting gap, "nothing is waiting on it" is a valid way to finish the sentence. Line 4 scores the same way across the planned gaps. The number of gaps is not scored: the page says the number is a result, not a target, so two good gaps can earn full points. A list with no blocking gap passes when the learner says so and describes where they looked. A learner with no interesting gaps has nothing to write for that part of line 3, and that is not a miss. On line 3, useful gaps may go into the order below the blocking ones, at no cost. An interesting gap placed in the order costs nothing as long as the blocking gaps still come first; mention it in the comment. A useful or interesting gap placed above a blocking one lands on the 3 rung. Parts B, C2, and D are blank on purpose at this stage. Read for meaning: if the idea is there in the learner's own words, give the points. A blank part is a 1 on its line, not a 0 on the item. Every rung below full names the part (A1 to A4, or C1) and the gap or slot it rests on. Each line names its part by letter and by header, so a learner can find it even if a label differs; the rungs, the gate, and "what makes it strong" do the same wherever a letter would stand alone (Melisa, 1 October). The ranks follow what the Dojo Lab depends on: the gap list first, then its link to the frame, then the order (Melisa, 2 October).

What makes it strong. Every part of your frame that could be wrong has a gap. (C1, My frame coming in, and A2, What I do not know yet.) Every blocking gap would change your frame. (A2, and A4, Why each planned gap matters to my frame.) Your blocking gaps come first in your order. (A3, My learning order.)

Open.
1. No minimum number of gaps, because the page says the number is a result, not a target. Check against the first batch whether learners with one or two gaps looked hard enough.
2. A grader cannot see whether the list was written without AI, or whether a mark is honest. Line 2 checks what can be seen: each blocking gap names what is waiting on it.
3. A grader does not have the Sprint 3 frame at hand, so line 5 checks that the frame in C1 is there and complete, not that it is an exact copy.
4. Page change, not yet made: the page says a gap whose sentence will not fill is a topic to rewrite or replace, but its own finished list has an interesting gap that nothing depends on. This record accepts that for useful and interesting gaps; the page should say so.
5. Page change, content settled, not yet made: the page has no "What to check in your work" box. Add one with the five full-points lines above, word for word, each with its points. To be done together with the boxes for the other Sprint 3 and Sprint 4 walkthroughs. The page is a sourced build, so this goes through the build map (docs/DOCUMENT_INTAKE.md).
6. All rungs are a best guess until the first batch is graded.
