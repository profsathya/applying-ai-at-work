# Sprint 1, Half Two: outline

## What half two is for

Half one ends with one committed problem and a runner-up. Half two turns that problem into
a first frame whose gaps are marked as gaps, widens it with AI, and hands Sprint 2 the
material it needs: the frame, the riskiest assumption, and the list of what is not yet known.

The disposition flips. Half one was look widely, hold loosely. Half two is go deep, take it
seriously. That flip should be said out loud on the first page.

Sprint 1's AI capacity is **widen**: open up a frame you are too close to see around. AI
appears once in half two, in the Dojo Lab, and the parked AI exchange folds into it.

## Each item, briefly

**8. The Problem Frame.** The concept treatment half one deliberately deferred. One page. The
seven components as a list (the goal it serves; the problem, specific enough that someone
could disagree; who is affected and what it costs them; how it is handled today and where
that falls short; what fixed looks like, as a changed state not a solution; what you are
assuming, marked confirmed or unverified; what you would need to find out next and who could
tell you). The filled-in example from the 24 August draft, on the running report case, so it
matches half one. One paragraph on why nobody fills it in top-down. No procedure; the doing
is on the next page.

**9. Your First Frame.** The think-first artifact, modelled on Sprint 4 V3's Name the Gap.
Opens with the material moved out of half one: your first guess at the goal underneath the
committed problem (the why-chain from the third activity, now run on your own problem) and
your assumptions, which are the (?) marks from Test and Commit written out. Then fill the
seven parts, rough, from your table and your Test and Commit submission. The rule: a gap you
mark is part of the deliverable, not a failure of it. The narrow no-AI reason applies here
in its clearest form: version 1 is your own read, and the next activity needs an independent
view to widen. This is the last place in the course that says "without AI."

**10. Dojo Lab: Widen the Frame.** Two moves in one session, both on your first frame.
First, widen: ask for three other ways to see the same situation, each with what it makes
visible, what it makes you miss, and who would have to be involved. Two follow-ups from the
19 August decision: which framing would be hardest to test in ten weeks, and does any framing
suggest the problem is already handled. Second, the parked AI exchange: hand it your frame
and ask which claims you are asserting rather than knowing. Record two or three challenges
and your response to each, in the specific-and-costly form the spine describes. Decide what
to keep, reject, or mark for verification. The prompt is given in full so this runs in any
chatbot if the Dojo is not ready. Depends on the Dojo and API key decision only for which
tool the page names.

**11. Goal Plan and Problem Frame.** The terminal artifact. Frame version 2, all seven
parts, with each assumption marked confirmed or unverified and one line on what changed from
version 1 and why, in the evidence-not-agreement register. Then the two hand-offs, each its
own section: the single riskiest assumption, with what would have to be true for the frame
to hold, which seeds Sprint 2's confirmation plan; and what you would need to find out next
and who could tell you, which seeds Sprint 3's stakeholder work. Closing line points forward:
in Sprint 2 you test this frame against alternatives and against what already exists, and
if it does not survive, that sprint says what to do. The switching message lives there, not
here. Name still open: Goal Plan and Problem Frame, Root Cause Analysis, or something else.

**12. Sprint 1 Reflection.** Same mechanism as Sprints 3 and 4: ai_activity, AI follow-up
questions, JSON upload. Prompt: put your first frame beside your Goal Plan and say what
changed, what AI contributed, and what you contributed that AI could not. The 19 August note
stands: this is the before-and-after record that is evidence for Superagency. Requires the
first frame to stay unedited, which the Carry Forward line must say.

## Decisions for Leslie

1. **Rhythm for week 2.** Two shapes. (A) The 4 September lineup: First Frame 15, Dojo Lab
   folded into the Goal Plan, Goal Plan 35, Reflection 10. Three graded items in week 2.
   (B) Week 1's rhythm extended: First Frame and Dojo Lab as own-your-progress at zero
   points, Goal Plan at 50, Reflection 10. Two graded items, one of them the floating
   reflection. B is consistent with the week-1 ruling and with the think-first work being
   ungraded in half one; A keeps the 4 September precedent with Sprint 4 V3's Name the Gap.
   Recommendation: B.
2. **Where the AI exchange lands.** Inside the Dojo Lab as its second move, as outlined, or
   dropped again. Folding it in keeps one AI touch and reuses the parked text.
3. **The terminal artifact's name.** Three names exist. This outline uses Goal Plan and
   Problem Frame because the Introduction's road map already does.
4. **Points.** The half-one total is 40. Half two carries 60 under either shape.

## Time

Half one as published is roughly four to four and a half hours by the same rough method used
before. Half two should aim under three: the Frame page fifteen minutes, First Frame
forty-five, Dojo Lab forty-five, Goal Plan sixty, Reflection twenty. The Introduction's
four-to-six-hour promise then needs to become six to seven, or the Frame page and the Dojo
Lab need to be shorter than this outline implies. Owed edit either way.

## What half two must not do

- Re-teach the four checks. Check 4's second test, one person who would talk to you, is
  answered by the Goal Plan's "who could tell you" section, and that is the only place a
  check reappears.
- State the exit window. Sprint 2 carries it.
- Use "stakeholder." Sprint 3 introduces the term; here it is "who could tell you."
- Add a peer activity. The "explain it to someone outside the course" line is the only peer
  move available until the pacing decision is made, and it fits the Goal Plan's closing.
