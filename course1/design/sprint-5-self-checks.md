# Sprint 5 Self-checks, v0.1

CIS 501, Sprint 5: Synthesize and Show Readiness (V2)
Two items, 50 points each. Passing: 35 points (70%).

Record shape and rules from `course1/design/self-check-guide.md`. Five lines per item, the guide's target. Each line names its part by number and header, and gives no counts, since length is not scored. Only the full-points lines go on the page; the rungs, ranks, tags, gate, and notes stay in this record.

Carried in from the Sprint 1 and Sprint 2 first batches: rungs only, no single-point deductions; score once; set scoring as every / all but one / two or more; a held or unchanged result, with why, is a full answer; readiness is judged by its reasons, not its verdict; AI-shaped writing is noted for a check-in, not deducted.

## Part 1. Integrated Problem Document Canvas Walkthrough

### Integrated Problem Document Canvas Walkthrough, 50 points

Submission: one Word file with Sections 1a to 4c. Section 1b, My current Problem Frame, is checked by the gate only. Lines 2 to 5 are judged across the items in their section.

| # | On the page (the full-points line) | Pts | 3 rung (pts) | 1 rung (pts) | Rank | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Section 1a, The problem, says in plain words what is happening, who it affects and what it costs them, and where things break down today, with no solution and no course terms. | 10 | The problem is there, but one of the three is missing, it only says something takes time, or a solution or course term slips in. Say what is happening, who pays, and where it breaks down, in words a stranger would use. (7) | A solution, a goal, or a summary of the course instead of the problem. Start from what is going wrong today, and for whom. (2) | 2 | judgment |
| 2 | Section 2, What I know, and how sure I am, names the things your problem rests on most, says what supports each one, marks each confirmed or inferred, and ends with what you don't know yet and why it matters. | 10 | The things your problem rests on are named and marked, but one has nothing behind it, or what you don't know yet is missing or does not say why it matters. Say what you saw, who you talked to, or what you read. (7) | Claims with nothing behind them, or everything marked confirmed. Inferred is an honest answer: say which parts you have not checked. (2) | 3 | honesty |

Total: 50. Passing: 35. Partial on every line gives 35.

Gate: an empty Section 1a, The problem, is not a pass, and no pasted frame in Section 1b, My current Problem Frame, is not a pass, whatever the other lines score. Any pasted frame passes the second gate: a learner without the Sprint 4 frame pastes their most recent one and brings it up to date, as the page asks.

Grading note. Lines 2 to 5 score once across the items in their section: full when every item passes, 3 when all but one pass and at least one passes, 1 when two or more miss or none passes. Score once: a weak Section 1a is scored on line 1 only, not carried into the other lines. Rungs only, no single-point deductions. Read for meaning: if the idea is there in the learner's own words, give the points. A blank part is a 1 on its line, not a 0 on the item. In Section 3, a moment where the problem held, with why it was kept, is a full answer; the page says so. In Section 4b, "I did not find one," with where one might show up, is a full answer. Confirmed or inferred, on lines 2 and 4, follows the page's own test: confirmed when the learner saw it, read it in a document or data, or heard it from the person it is about; inferred when it is only their read, or one person's explanation of why other people do something. Note a wrong mark in the comment; score it on the line only when most marks are wrong. Missing work: a learner who names a missing piece from an earlier sprint and works from what they have, as the page asks, is not marked down for the gap; a Dojo exchange described from memory and said to be from memory counts. Writing as if the piece is there is scored on its line. Overlap between Section 1a and Section 1b is expected and not scored. Length is not scored, including the number of sentences in Section 1a; a part of a step left unanswered is (Melisa, 17 November). Whether the problem is ready for CIS 502 is not scored here; that is the Readiness Report's question. Every rung below full names the section, by number and header, and the item it rests on. The ranks follow what comes next: the requirements and the conflict first, because CIS 502 starts from them and the Dojo Lab tests the conflict, then the problem as a reader first meets it (Melisa, 17 November).

What makes it strong. The problem in 1a is your frame, said plainly. (Section 1a, The problem, and Section 1b, My current Problem Frame.) What you are still guessing shows up as inferred requirements. (Section 2, What I know, and how sure I am, and Section 4a, What a solution must do.) Each thing that already exists misses at least one requirement, which shows the gap a solution has to close. (Section 4a and Section 4c, What already exists or has been tried.)

Total: 50. Passing: 35. Partial on every line gives 35.

Gate: a report with no direction picked in Part 5, My direction, is not a pass, whatever the other lines score. CIS 502 starts from the direction (Melisa, 17 November). A missing verdict is scored on line 1, not gated.

Grading note. The verdict itself is not scored; its reasons are. Any of the three calls earns full on line 1 when the reasons are there, and "not ready yet" earns full when it gives a clear reason and a next step, as the page asks. A call that changes between Part 0 and Part 5 is not better or worse than one that holds; the line asks why. "Before you reread" and "left as you first wrote it" cannot be seen by a grader and are not scored. Line 3 scores Parts 2 and 3 as a set: full when both pass; 3 when one part is weak, such as no moment, or Part 2 missing what caused a shift or the AI answer; 1 when one part is blank or both are weak. Skipping a whole part costs more than doing it thinly (Melisa, 17 November). In Parts 2 and 3, "little changed, and why" is a full answer, as the page says. The AI questions in Part 2 are scored on line 3 (Melisa, 17 November): "I did not tackle anything with AI I could not have done alone," with why, is a full answer. The page needs a matching edit: "As you write, think about two questions" becomes "Answer these two questions." In Part 4, "nothing changed in my plan," with why, is a full answer. Line 2 scores across the strengths and needs named: full when every one passes, 3 when all but one do, 1 when two or more miss. Score once: a missing moment in one part is scored on that part's line only. Rungs only, no single-point deductions. Read for meaning: if the idea is there in the learner's own words, give the points. A blank part is a 1 on its line, not a 0 on the item. Length is not scored. Writing that looks AI-shaped is noted for a check-in, not deducted. Every rung below full names the part, by number and header, and the moment or item it rests on. The ranks follow what comes next: the verdict first, because it is what the report is for, then the direction and the gaps that CIS 502 starts from.

What makes it strong. Your verdict rests on what you still need to learn. (Part 0, My verdict, before I look, Part 4, What I still need to learn, and the verdict in Part 5, My direction.) The direction you pick goes after your top gap. (Part 4 and Part 5.) Your growth is shown by moments from your own work, not by claims. (Part 2, How I've grown in how I work, and Part 3, How my thinking has changed.)
