# Course 1 Structure

## Week 1 — Orientation

Participants meet the cohort, understand course expectations, choose their workplace problem, and identify initial stakeholders. This is the autonomy moment: they are choosing what they will work on for 10 weeks. The problem must be real — from their actual workplace, affecting real people, with real consequences.

Specific orientation activities are for the team to design.

## Sprints 1–4 (Weeks 2–9, two weeks each)

Each sprint develops the participant's capability to frame their chosen problem. Across the four sprints, participants move from an initial vague concern toward a clearly articulated, stakeholder-validated problem statement.

Specific sprint themes, activities, and assessments are for the team to design. The following constraints apply to all sprints:

- Each sprint should include a real stakeholder touchpoint — an actual conversation with someone in the participant's organization, not a simulated interaction.
- Each sprint should produce a visible artifact that demonstrates growing capability.
- Each sprint should deliver value the participant can feel immediately, not value promised for later.

## Week 10 — Capstone

Participants demonstrate their problem framing to peers and stakeholders. This is the competence moment: visible evidence of what they can now do that they could not before. The capstone should feel like a natural culmination of the sprint arc, not a sudden test.

Specific capstone format is for the team to design.

---

This structure is prescribed because it is load-bearing for the audience and SDT design. Working professionals need orientation before being asked to produce, a steady sprint cadence that respects their time constraints, and a capstone that makes their growth visible. The content within each sprint is for the team to develop.
