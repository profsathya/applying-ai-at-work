# Welcome video, v0.3

About 560 words, which is three and a half to four minutes at a conversational pace. Record together on Zoom, speaker view, one take straight through with one restart allowed. Small stumbles stay in. Jeremy trims the ends and adds the final text frame.

Square brackets mark things to fill in or decide. Everything else is a suggestion to rewrite in your own voice. Keep each turn under 30 seconds.

## 1. Welcome (Tacey, 30 seconds)

Hi, and welcome to Reframing Problems with AI. I'm Tacey [last name], and I'm your instructor for this course. [One sentence about Tacey: what she teaches at De Anza, or why she took this course on.]

Here is what the next ten weeks are about. You are going to pick one real problem from your own work or life, and understand it properly: who it affects and what it costs them, what is already being done about it, what you are assuming, and what you still need to learn. That depth is the point. It is what lets you work on a problem, and it is what lets you explain the problem to AI well enough to get real help with it. You will use AI the whole way through, and you will find out where it helps and where the thinking has to be yours.

I didn't build this course alone. Three people designed and built it with me, and you will be meeting them in office hours and one-on-ones. Let me hand over so they can introduce themselves.

## 2. The course team: one point each (25 seconds each)

Tacey introduced the three of you as the people who built the course with her. Each of you says your name, one line on what you did, and one thing a learner needs to hear in week one. Nobody says "come to me for this part": the team supports the whole course, and section 4 gives one way in.

**Leslie.** I'm Leslie Kern. I worked on the early sprints, where you find your problem. The one thing I want you to hear: pick something from your own life that you actually bump into every week. Not the biggest problem you can think of, the one you know from the inside. Small and real beats big and impressive every time in this course.

**Melisa.** I'm Melisa Villafuerte Mendoza. I worked on the middle sprints, where you take your problem out to the people it affects. The one thing I want you to hear: this course will keep asking you to go and talk to real people. That is the part most of us would rather skip, and it is the part where your understanding of the problem actually changes. [Rewrite in your own words.]

**Jeremy.** I'm Jeremy [last name]. I built the Dojo, the AI workspace you will use in every sprint. The one thing I want you to hear: write it yourself first, then bring it to the Dojo. The AI here reads what you wrote and pushes on it. It is a sparring partner, not an answer machine, and it is only as good as what you give it. [Rewrite in your own words.]

## 3. The first-run message (Tacey, 40 seconds)

One thing we want to say plainly. This is the first time this course has run. The subject, working with AI, changes month to month, and there was no course like this to copy from. We built it carefully, and we are going to adjust it as we go, based on what we see from you.

That means you are the first group through it, and what you tell us changes the course. If a page is confusing, if an activity takes three times longer than we said, if the AI does something strange, tell us before you have spent an evening on it. The best way is to come to office hours or book a one-on-one. Those conversations are how we find out what is really happening, and they are the part of this we are most looking forward to.

## 4. How to reach us, and where to start (Tacey, 30 seconds)

There is one way in, and it reaches all of us. Office hours are [day and time, and the Zoom link is in Canvas under Help and Resources]; whoever is there that day can help with any part of the course. One-on-ones: [how to book]. For anything else, Canvas Inbox, and we will get back to you within [a day, or whatever the team commits to].

Start with Sprint 0. It is short: it sets up your AI workspace and walks you through how the course runs. Then Sprint 1 opens on [date].

## 5. Close (all, 15 seconds)

[One line each, or Tacey alone.] We are glad you are here. See you in office hours.

Office hours: [days and times]. Book a one-on-one: [link]. Questions: Canvas Inbox. Start here: Sprint 0.

## Notes

- Tacey is the instructor; the other three are the course team. Say it that way and do not explain the organisations.
- One team, one way in. No segment routes learners to a person or a sprint. Office hours are staffed by whoever is on, for any part of the course. Every team member needs to be able to field any question once the course is live.
- Say the first-run message once, in section 3, and do not apologise elsewhere. Confidence plus honesty, not hedging.
