# Rubric: Stakeholder Map Canvas Walkthrough

CIS 501, Sprint 3: Integrate People and Context (V3)
50 points. Passing: 35 points (70%).
Status: first pass, 1 October 2026.

## Rubric

| # | Full points | Pts | Partial | Minimal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Stakeholder tables** | | | | |
| 1 | All four stakeholders have a Role and a relationship that say how they connect to the problem. | 7 | Two or three stakeholders have a Role and a relationship that say how they connect to the problem. (5) | One stakeholder has a Role and a relationship that say how they connect to the problem. (1) |
| 2 | For all four stakeholders, impact and authority are answered, with different answers or a note on why they are the same. | 7 | For two or three stakeholders, impact and authority are answered, with different answers or a note on why they are the same. (5) | For one stakeholder, impact and authority are answered, with different answers or a note on why they are the same. (1) |
| 3 | For all four stakeholders, the Need describes what the person cares about, not a tool or fix. | 6 | For two or three stakeholders, the Need describes what the person cares about, not a tool or fix. (4) | For one stakeholder, the Need describes what the person cares about, not a tool or fix. (1) |
| 4 | For all four stakeholders, Constraints and Next question are answered. Short answers count. | 4 | For two or three stakeholders, Constraints and Next question are answered. (3) | For one stakeholder, Constraints and Next question are answered. (1) |
| 5 | Every answered field is marked Confirmed or Inferred, with a short reason. | 9 | Most answered fields are marked Confirmed or Inferred, with a short reason. (6) | A few answered fields are marked Confirmed or Inferred, with a short reason. (2) |
| **Assumption blocks** | | | | |
| 6 | Two or three assumption blocks are included, each with all four lines answered. | 6 | Two or three assumption blocks are included, with one or two lines missing in total. (4) | One assumption block is included, or three or more lines are missing. (1) |
| 7 | In every assumption block, the question asks what happened or what the person does, and a change is named. | 6 | In some assumption blocks, but not all, the question asks what happened or what the person does, and a change is named. (4) | In one assumption block at most, the question asks what happened or what the person does. (1) |
| **First contact** | | | | |
| 8 | A first choice and a backup are named, with a reason for talking to the first choice first. | 5 | A first choice and a backup are named, but the reason for talking to the first choice first is unclear. (4) | A first choice or a backup is missing, or no reason is given. (1) |
| | **Total** | **50** | **35** | **9** |

**Grader note.** Read for meaning. If the idea is there in the student's own words, give the points. A blank row earns 0.

**What makes it strong.** The Inferred fields are the ones the assumption blocks go after. The first choice is the person who can answer the top assumption.

**Grading, in the open.** Do most of each part and you pass. Do all of it for full points. A partial comment doubles as the nudge: which field, which person, what is missing.

## Principles used

1. **Two layers.** The short "before you submit" box is the student's quick check. The rubric is the full grading breakdown behind it.
2. **Built on Sathya's Self-check rule and Leslie's Sprint 1 example.** Neutral, observable lines; points per row; full, partial, and minimal levels.
3. **Grade only what is on the page.** No row depends on what the student knows or could do outside the assignment.
4. **Read for meaning, not wording.** Short or plain answers earn the points if the idea is there.
5. **Points follow effort.** Rows are weighted by how much students actually write: about 66% stakeholder tables, 24% assumption blocks, 10% first contact.
6. **Each thing is graded once.** No blank field loses points in two rows.
7. **Clear on full, generous on partial.** The full-points line states exact counts. Partial covers "most of it." Minimal is for real gaps.
8. **Every cell is a full statement.** Numbers are written out, and no cell relies on another to make sense.
9. **Partial everywhere equals a pass.** Partial is set near 70% of each row, so a student who does most of each part reaches 35 of 50.
