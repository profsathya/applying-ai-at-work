# Sprint 2: outline

## What Sprint 2 is for

Sprint 2 receives a framed problem with its gaps marked and does two things to it. It tests
the frame against alternatives and against what already exists in the world, which is Check
2 widened with AI. And it produces a confirmation plan: what is still unverified, who could
verify it, and what question would do the verifying. Sprint 3 executes that plan with a real
person.

The AI capacity is **direct**: supply context, constraints, and standards, then judge what
comes back. V1's Context, Choices, Confirmation vocabulary is worth keeping; it is the one
piece of V1 Sprint 2 that teaches rather than describes.

Sprint 2 is the sprint where switching problems is free, and it should say so here, where it
is actionable, rather than in Sprint 1: if the widening shows the problem is already handled,
not real, or not current, go back to your runner-up from Test and Commit. That is the
finding the sprint was designed to produce, not a failure.

## What Sprint 2 receives and hands on

Receives from Sprint 1: the committed problem and runner-up; frame version 2; the riskiest
assumption; the list of unverified claims; the alternative framings considered in the Dojo
Lab.

Hands to Sprint 3: frame version 3; the confirmation plan; candidate people who could
verify, which Sprint 3's stakeholder map opens by asking for; the questions drafted for them.

## Each item, briefly

**2. Introduction.** Keep V1's same-task-two-ways example, the late-deliverable email; it is
the best thing in V1. Add the mechanism from the spine, stated as a reason: fluent output is
hard to judge without an independent view to check it against, so you form yours first.
Define Context, Choices, Confirmation once. State the switching rule. Standard sections.

**3. What Handles This Today.** Before any AI: the "X handles this today, but it does not
___" line from Check 2, now taken seriously. Name the mechanism, the person absorbing it,
and where it stops working. Then your guess at what others in your situation do about it.
This is the independent view the rest of the sprint judges AI against.

**4. Dojo Lab: What Already Exists.** Directed, with the 3Cs made concrete: the context you
supply (your frame and your "handles this today" line), the choices you ask for (three ways
this problem is handled elsewhere, with tradeoffs), the confirmation you name (which of
those you could check, and how). This is where Check 2 gets its downstream home. Prompt
given in full; runs in any chatbot.

**6. AI Output Assessment and Pushback.** Take one output from the Dojo Lab. Judge it against
the standard you set before you asked. Write the pushback in the form the spine requires:
what you rejected, why, and what you lost by rejecting it. Then what you kept and what you
marked for verification. This is the graded item because it is where directing AI becomes
observable.

**7. Dojo Lab: Test the Frame.** The alternatives move. Take the two strongest alternative
framings from Sprint 1's Dojo Lab, or ask for two new ones, and for each say what it would
change about who pays, what is handled today, and what you would need to find out. Decide
whether your frame survives, shifts, or breaks. If it breaks, this is where the runner-up
comes back.

**8. Confirmation Plan.** The terminal artifact. Frame version 3 on top. Then a table, one
row per unverified claim: the claim, who could confirm or complicate it, the question you
would ask, in the evidence-not-approval form Sprint 3 teaches. Riskiest assumption first.
Two or three people named as candidates for Sprint 3, with a line on why each. If the
problem changed, one section saying what the widening showed and which runner-up you took.

**9. Reflection.** ai_activity, JSON upload. Prompt: where did your judgment change what AI
gave you, and where did AI change your frame. Both directions, with one example each.

## Dependencies and risks

- **The AI proxy is down and the Dojo question is unresolved.** Sprint 2 cannot be tested
  by the 15th if its AI activities depend on the guided mechanism. Every AI activity in this
  outline gives its prompt in full and accepts a pasted transcript, so it runs in any
  chatbot. The reflection uses the course's ai_activity mechanism and is exposed to the
  proxy outage; if it is not fixed, the reflection falls back to text entry.
- **V1's peer exchange** was an AI activity wearing the word "peer." Not carried forward.
  The verify line, show one AI output and your pushback to someone outside the course, goes
  into the Assessment item as an optional closing step.
- **Sprint 3 V2's opening** asks for at least three stakeholders the learner already believes
  are connected. The Confirmation Plan's candidate people satisfy that directly. Melisa
  should know the hand-off exists.
