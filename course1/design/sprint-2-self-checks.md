# Sprint 2 Self-checks, v0.1

Same shape as Sprint 1 v0.2: five lines per item, one completeness line and four quality tests, points set by what each line carries and adding up to 50. Rung labels: 5 = full points (on the page), 3 = partial, 1 = minimal (off the page, used in grading comments). Priority H, M, L. Tag: completeness, specificity, honesty, judgment.

Each list is written against the page's existing per-task criteria, which it replaces on the page once the renderer question (item 39) is settled.

## Dig into your assumptions, 50 points

Submission: one Word file, one four-row table per assumption from Part 6 (three required, up to seven).

| # | On the page (the 5 line) | Points | 3 rung | 1 rung | Priority | Tag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Every assumption in Part 6 has its own table, pasted as it stands in the frame, not softened. | 8 | Most assumptions have a table; one or two reworded into something safer. | Fewer than three tables, or assumptions rewritten past recognition. | H | completeness |
| 2 | The questions behind each assumption ask something the assumption takes for granted, not the assumption with a question mark. | 10 | Real questions on most tables; one or two restate the assumption. | Every question restates its assumption. | H | specificity |
| 3 | The different answers on each table include at least one that would change or end the problem, and the answers vary from table to table. | 12 | One table's answers all lead to "so I proceed," or the same three answers reused. | Answers missing, or one formula on every table. | H | judgment |
| 4 | Every risk rating gives a reason that says why that answer would or would not shut the problem down. | 12 | Ratings present, some reasons missing or restating the rating. | Ratings with no reasons. | H | judgment |
| 5 | The ratings are mixed and match their reasons: not every row high, not every row low. | 8 | Ratings all one level with reasons that mostly support them. | Every row the same rating with no reasons, or ratings that contradict their reasons. | M | honesty |

Total: 50.

**What makes it strong.** The answer that would end the problem is the one the learner least wants to hear, and it is written down anyway. The high-risk rows are the ones Part 6's Status column will carry into the Problem Frame page, so a strong walk makes the taking-stock verdict almost write itself.

**Grading, in the open.** A table at the 3 rung on lines 3 or 4 gets the comment "which answer would make you drop this, and how likely is it," which is the Dojo Lab's opening question.

## What solutions already exist, 50 points

Submission: one Word file, the three-column table with at least three rows.

| # | On the page (the 5 line) | Points | 3 rung | 1 rung | Priority | Tag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | At least three rows, each a thing that already exists (a tool, a practice, a workaround, a person), not something you would build. | 10 | Three rows, one of them a solution you would build. | Fewer than three rows, or mostly things you would build. | H | completeness |
| 2 | "How it works" is written from what you have actually seen or read, and says "cannot tell yet" where you have not looked. | 12 | Mostly seen; one row written from how it probably works. | Rows written from guesses dressed as facts. | H | honesty |
| 3 | "What using it here would involve" names what would have to change for the people in your frame, not whether you like the option. | 12 | Names a change for some rows; others give an opinion of the option. | Opinions or blanks in place of what would change. | H | specificity |
| 4 | The rows include something close to home, how people already cope, as well as something from outside the situation. | 8 | Only outside tools, or only workarounds. | One kind of row only, and fewer than three. | M | judgment |
| 5 | No row is marked as the answer; open questions and blanks are left standing. | 8 | One row favoured before looking, questions kept. | A row chosen as the solution, blanks filled with guesses. | M | judgment |

Total: 50.

**What makes it strong.** Column 3 is where Sprint 2's Part 3c comes from, so a strong column 3 names people and the habit or step they would have to take on. A row that says "cannot tell yet" in column 2 and names who could tell you is better than a confident row written from a product page.

**Grading, in the open.** A column 2 at the 3 rung gets the comment "which of these have you actually seen working, and where."

## Problem Frame: taking stock of where you are, 50 points

Submission: three boxes in one Canvas text entry. Five lines in box order.

| # | On the page (the 5 line) | Points | 3 rung | 1 rung | Priority | Tag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Box 1 names at least one person and what fixing this would ask of them (a step, a habit, time, or a decision), drawn from column 3 of your What exists table. | 10 | A person named, the ask vague, or not tied to column 3. | No person, or a solution described instead of what it asks. | H | specificity |
| 2 | Box 2 holds all seven parts, dated, Part 3c now filled, and every row of Part 6 with its Status column filled and a reason for each rating. | 12 | A part or a few Status entries missing, or ratings without reasons. | Several parts missing, 3c blank, or Part 6 unchanged from Sprint 1. | H | completeness |
| 3 | Part 6 has at least one assumption rated high risk and unconfirmed. | 6 | A high-risk row marked confirmed without saying how. | No high-risk row at all. | M | honesty |
| 4 | Box 3 gives one of the three verdicts, and the reasons draw on the risk ratings, what exists, and what the change would ask. | 12 | A verdict with reasons from one of the three sources. | A verdict with no reasons, or reasons from feeling alone. | H | judgment |
| 5 | Box 3 says what is still uncertain and what would settle it. A "depends on" names what and who. A no names the runner-up. | 10 | Uncertainty named without what would settle it. | Nothing uncertain named, or a "depends on" with no condition. | H | honesty |

Total: 50.

**What makes it strong.** The verdict's reasons point at specific rows: a risk rating in Part 6, a row in What exists, a person in box 1. A no earns full marks when its reasoning is this specific, and the page says so. "What would settle it" is the thing Sprint 3's stakeholder conversation goes and asks.

**Grading, in the open.** A box 3 at the 3 rung gets the comment "which row in Part 6 or What exists is this reason standing on," which is where Sprint 3 starts.
