## Part 1. Stakeholder Map Canvas Walkthrough

### Stakeholder Map Canvas Walkthrough, 50 points

Submission: one Word file with four stakeholder tables, two or three assumption blocks, and the first-contact table. Lines 2 to 5 are judged across all four stakeholder tables. Lines 6 and 7 are judged across the assumption blocks. The outreach log is optional and is not scored.

| # | On the page (the full-points line) | Pts | 3 rung (pts) | 1 rung (pts) | Rank | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Four stakeholders, with all seven fields complete for each. | 6 | Three stakeholders with all seven fields, or four with up to three fields blank. Fill in what is missing, even with short answers. (4) | Two stakeholders, or more than three fields left blank. Go back to Part 3a and Part 6 of your Problem Frame for the people you already named. (1) | 1 | completeness |
| 2 | Each stakeholder is defined by their relationship to your problem. | 6 | Three stakeholders are defined by their relationship to your problem; one gives a job title alone. Say what that person does or sees in your problem. (4) | Two or more stakeholders have a job title with no relationship attached. Say what each person does, decides, or sees in your problem. (1) | 2 | specificity |
| 3 | For each stakeholder, impact says how the problem affects them, and authority says what they have a say over. | 5 | In three tables, impact and authority each say what they are about; in one, they repeat each other, or a level such as "high" or "low" has nothing attached. Ask how hard the problem hits this person, and separately, what they can change. (4) | In two or more tables, impact and authority repeat each other, or a level has nothing attached. They are separate facts: the person who feels the problem most is often the one least able to change it. (1) | 6 | specificity |
| 4 | Each Need says what the stakeholder cares about. | 6 | Three Needs say what the stakeholder cares about; one names a tool or a fix. Ask what that person wants to protect or avoid. (4) | Two or more Needs name a tool or a fix, which is your solution in the stakeholder's voice. Start from what each person wants to protect or avoid. (1) | 5 | judgment |
| 5 | In each stakeholder table, every field except "Next question to ask" is marked Confirmed or Inferred, with a specific reason. | 9 | Up to six of the 24 fields are missing a mark or a reason. More than six fields have no mark or reason, or all say Confirmed with no evidence. Marking a guess Inferred is not a weakness; your conversation will test it. (2) | 4 | honesty |
| 6 | Two or three assumption blocks, each with all four lines, and "What a different answer changes" names a specific change to your frame or your plan. | 8 | Two or three blocks, and at least one names a specific change. Another is general, such as "I would rethink it," or has a line left blank. Name what you would do differently: a different problem, different people, or a different next conversation. Pick an assumption that would change your next step if it turned out wrong. (2) | 3 | judgment |

Another asks what the stakeholder thinks of your idea. Rewrite it as "Walk me through the last time this happened." (4) | None of your assumption questions asks about a real experience, a past decision, or a tradeoff. They ask what the stakeholder thinks of your idea. Ask what happened, or what the person did. (1) | 7 | specificity |
| 8 | A first choice and a backup are named, and the reason says why this person comes first. | 5 | A first choice and a backup are named, but the reason says why the person matters, not why they come first. Tie it to your highest-stakes assumption, or to where your evidence is thinnest. (4) | The first choice or the backup is missing, or no reason is given. Name both people, and say why your first choice comes before anyone else. (1) | 8 | judgment |

Total: 50. Passing: 35. Partial on every line gives 36.

Gate: fewer than two stakeholder tables is not a pass, and a map with no assumption block is not a pass, whatever the other lines score.

Grading note. Lines 2 to 4 score once across the four stakeholder tables: full when all four pass, 3 when three do, 1 when two or fewer do. Line 5 scores once across the 24 fields to mark (six in each table; the question field is left out): full when all are marked with a reason, 3 when up to six are missing a mark or a reason, 1 when more than six are. Lines 6 and 7 score once across the assumption blocks. Read for meaning: if the idea is there in the learner's own words, give the points. A blank part is a 1 on its line, not a 0 on the item. A group, or a role without a name yet, counts as one stakeholder. On line 5, the "Next question to ask" field does not need a mark: a mark there, "Not applicable," or a blank all pass. "Partly confirmed" passes when it says which part is confirmed and which is a guess. On line 7, a question about what would make the person do or skip something passes, as long as it does not ask them to approve the idea; the page lists a similar question as strong. A blank Assumption 3 is not a miss. Every rung below full names the table and the field it rests on, or the assumption block and the line. On line 5, name the tables that have the gaps, not each field, and ask the learner to review the Confirmed or Inferred column there. The ranks put the count and the relationship first, then the assumption blocks: every later step in the sprint is built on the four stakeholder tables, so a gap there is the first thing the learner should hear (Melisa, 1 October).

What makes it strong. The fields marked Inferred are the ones the assumption blocks go after. The question in each block grows out of a "Next question to ask" in a stakeholder table. The first choice is the person who can answer the top assumption.

Open.
1. Eight lines. The guide's target is five. Lines 3 and 4 could merge, and so could 6 and 7, if the first batch shows they fail together.
2. Lines 2 to 4 use the stricter standard: three of four tables for the 3 rung (Melisa, 1 October). Check against the first batch whether this is too strict.
3. Page changes agreed on 1 October, not yet made. The final check box is replaced by the eight full-points lines above, each with its points. The sentence that tells learners to mark the question field Confirmed or Inferred is replaced by: The question field does not need a mark. You can write "Not applicable" there. The page is a sourced build, so a hand edit fails the source fidelity check; the change goes through the build map and a rebuild (docs/DOCUMENT_INTAKE.md).
4. Line 1 has a small space between its rungs: three stakeholders with one or two fields blank. Decide where that lands when the first batch comes in.
5. All rungs are a best guess until the first batch is graded.

## Part 2. Stakeholder Conversation Canvas Walkthrough

Record shape and rules from `course1/design/self-check-guide.md`. This replaces the v0.1 rubric for this item, which had ten lines. Five lines, the guide's target. Only the full-points lines go on the page; the rungs, ranks, tags, gate, and notes stay in this record.

### Stakeholder Conversation Canvas Walkthrough, 50 points

Submission: one Word file with Sections A to D, three validation questions, and the updated Problem Frame. In line 1, the three questions are judged as a set.

| # | On the page (the full-points line) | Pts | 3 rung (pts) | 1 rung (pts) | Rank | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | All five fields in Section A are complete, and each of your three validation questions asks about an experience, a decision, or a tradeoff. | 8 | The assumption is named and two questions ask about an experience, a decision, or a tradeoff, but a field is blank, or one question asks for an opinion of your idea. Fill in the field, or ask what happened. (6) | The assumption is missing, or fewer than two questions ask about an experience, a decision, or a tradeoff. Carry the assumption over from your Stakeholder Map, and ask what happened. (2) | 5 | specificity |
| 2 | All three rows in Section B are complete, and the first row records the stakeholder's words, as close as you can capture them. | 10 | The stakeholder's words are in the first row, but another row is blank, or your own interpretation is mixed in. Move your reading to Sections C and D. (7) | The first row is mostly your interpretation, or two rows are blank. Write down what the stakeholder said, as close to their words as you can. (2) | 3 | honesty |
| 3 | All four rows in Section C are complete, and each one connects to what the stakeholder said in Section B. | 8 | Three rows connect to what the stakeholder said, but one is blank or does not connect. Say which part of the conversation it comes from. (6) | Two or more rows are blank, or the rows do not connect to what the stakeholder said. Go back to your notes and name what gave you each impression. (2) | 4 | specificity |
| 4 | All three rows in Section D are complete, and the first row says what changed in your Problem Frame and why, or why it held. | 12 | The first row says what changed, or that the frame held, but it gives no reason, or another row is blank. Name what the stakeholder said that led you there. (8) | The first row does not say whether the frame changed or held, or two rows are blank. State one or the other, and point to what you heard. (2) | 2 | honesty |
| 5 | All seven parts of your Problem Frame are pasted, and Parts 6 and 7 are brought up to date with what you heard. | 12 | The frame is pasted, but one or two parts are missing, or only one of Parts 6 and 7 is updated. Paste the whole frame, and update both parts. (8) | Four or fewer parts are pasted, or neither Part 6 nor Part 7 is updated. Paste the whole frame, and mark what this conversation tested. (2) | 1 | judgment |

Total: 50. Passing: 35. Partial on every line gives 35.

Gate: a blank Section B is not a pass, and a report with no pasted Problem Frame is not a pass, whatever the other lines score. A learner whose conversation has not happened yet resubmits after talking with their stakeholder, or with a backup stakeholder.

Grading note. In line 1, the three questions score as a set: all three pass for full, two for the 3 rung, one or none for the 1 rung. Read for meaning: if the idea is there in the learner's own words, give the points. A blank part is a 1 on its line, not a 0 on the item. A privacy-safe paraphrase counts as the stakeholder's words. "Nothing" is a valid answer for "Anything they offered to follow up on" and for "What they challenged or complicated." On line 4, a frame that held, with a reason, earns full points: no change is a valid result, and "nothing moved," with a reason, is a valid answer for what moved from Inferred to Confirmed. "What I hope to learn" under each question is a thinking step and is not scored; a blank there does not cost points. A written conversation counts: the stakeholder's written answers are their words for the first row of Section B, with sensitive details paraphrased. The points lean toward Section D and the Problem Frame because Sprint 4 starts from them. On line 3, look for a real connection to the conversation, not matching words. On line 5, Part 6 is up to date when each assumption this conversation tested shows a status; "wrong" is a valid status, beside confirmed and unverified. Every rung below full names the section and the row it rests on. The ranks follow what Sprint 4 depends on: the updated Problem Frame first, then Section D, because Sprint 4 builds on Parts 6 and 7 and on what still needs validation (Melisa, 1 October).

What makes it strong. At least one question goes straight at the assumption in Section A. The first impressions in Section C come from what the stakeholder said in Section B. What Section D says changed shows up in the pasted Problem Frame, and what Section D says still needs validation matches Part 7.

Open.
1. Five lines, down from ten in v0.1 (Melisa, 1 October). Each line checks that a section is complete and one sign of quality, so a line can drop for either reason. Check against the first batch whether that hides anything.
2. The grader cannot see the Problem Frame as it was before the conversation, so line 5 checks that Parts 6 and 7 are up to date, not what changed.
3. Page change, not yet made: the "What to check in your work" box has lines a grader cannot see, such as "sharpened, not just copied" and "right after the conversation." Replace the box with the five lines above and their points. The page is a sourced build, so this goes through the build map (docs/DOCUMENT_INTAKE.md).
4. Page change, not yet made: the page tells learners to mark each tested assumption in Part 6 confirmed or unverified. An assumption the conversation proved false has no label. This record accepts "wrong"; the page should offer it too.
5. All rungs are a best guess until the first batch is graded.
