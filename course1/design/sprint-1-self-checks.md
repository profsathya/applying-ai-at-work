# Sprint 1 Self-checks, v0.3

The rules are in `self-check-guide.md`. Rung points follow its rule: a 3 earns about 70 percent of the line, a 1 about 20 percent. Passing is 35 of 50. Lines are written for the learner; rungs are written as the sentence the learner receives in the grading comment.

## First frames, 50 points

Submission: one Word file with three to five frame tables. Lines 2 to 6 are judged across every frame submitted: full when every frame passes the line, 3 when most do, 1 when few or none. Only line 1 counts frames.

Gate: fewer than two frames does not pass, whatever the other lines score. The grading line says so.

| # | On the page | Pts | 3 rung (pts) | 1 rung (pts) | Rank | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | You have at least three frames, and every part of each one is filled in or says "I do not know yet." | 10 | Two frames, every part filled in or marked. Two gives you a runner-up; three gives the Dojo Lab a real choice. (7) | One frame, or frames with most parts blank. (2) | 6 | completeness |
| 2 | In each frame, Part 2 says what is going wrong and who it is going wrong for, in one or two sentences. | 8 | Something is going wrong, but it is not clear what, or for whom. Say both. (6) | Part 2 is a task, a complaint, or a solution, not a problem. Go back to your table and start from the gap. (2) | 1 | judgment |
| 3 | In each frame, Part 5 describes what would be different if the problem in Part 2 were fixed, without saying how. | 8 | A changed situation is described, even if a fix is named with it or it does not quite match Part 2. Describe the day after this problem is gone. (6) | Part 5 is a solution, such as "build an app," or is missing. Describe what would be different, not how. (2) | 3 | judgment |
| 4 | In each frame, Part 3a names everyone who feels this problem, and Part 3b says what it costs each of them: time, money, mistakes, or stress. | 8 | Only one person or role is named, or some named people have no cost. A problem usually touches more than one person: who else feels it, and what does it cost them? (6) | One vague group, such as "everyone," or a cost with nobody attached. Start from who feels this and say what each one loses. (2) | 4 | specificity |
| 5 | In each frame, Part 4 says how people deal with this problem now and where that stops working. | 6 | How people deal with it now is there, but not where it falls short. Say what it does not cover. (4) | A fix or a "should" instead of what people do today. Describe the current habit first. (1) | 5 | specificity |
| 6 | In each frame, Part 6 lists at least four assumptions, and each one says which part it comes from and who could tell you whether it is true. | 10 | Four assumptions, but some do not say which part or who could tell you. Each line needs both: that is how Sprint 2 knows where to look. (7) | Fewer than four, or none says who could tell you. Reread parts 2 to 5 and mark every sentence you have not seen for yourself. (2) | 2 | honesty |

Total 50. Passing 35. Partial on every line gives 36.

**Grading note.** Every rung below full names the frame and the part it rests on. Read for meaning: if the idea is there in the learner's own words, give the points. A blank part is a 1 on its line, not a 0 on the frame. If a learner's problem genuinely touches one person and they say so, line 4 earns full.

**What makes it strong.** The cost in 3b is the thing Part 5 would change. The assumptions in Part 6 are the sentences in 2 to 5 the learner has not seen for themselves. Part 5 is the problem in Part 2, gone. Three frames that differ in kind (a process, a habit, a decision) are stronger than three versions of one situation.

**Open, for the first-batch revision.** Whether line 2's 1 rung (task, complaint, or solution) is the common miss, or something else is. Whether "only one person named" is the right trigger for line 4's 3 rung.

## Problem Frame, 50 points

Submission: three responses in one Canvas text entry, under the page's three headings, which become Step 1, Step 2, and Step 3 (page edit, 2 October) so that they do not collide with the frame's own Parts 1 to 7. Grading line 4 needs the learner's First frames submission open beside this one; that is a requirement for Jeremy's grader (list item 46).

Gate: no pasted frame in Step 2 is not a pass, whatever Steps 1 and 3 score.

| # | On the page | Pts | 3 rung (pts) | 1 rung (pts) | Rank | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Your answer to Step 1, Which problem you selected and why, answers all four questions and includes your runner-up. | 7 | Three of the four, and one of them is the runner-up. Your runner-up is what you go back to if this problem does not survive Sprint 2. (5) | Two or fewer answered, or no runner-up. (1) | 6 | completeness |
| 2 | In Step 1, your explanation of why this problem is good for this course points to a reason beyond your interest, such as the people you can reach or a question you can actually test. | 6 | A reason is given, even if it is not tied to the frame or the testing. (4) | Missing, or your interest only. (1) | 5 | judgment |
| 3 | In Step 2, your Problem Frame is pasted whole, all seven parts in your own words, with 3c left blank. | 10 | The frame is pasted with at most one part missing or in the Dojo's words. Make every part yours. (7) | Several parts missing, or the frame summarized instead of pasted. This is your Problem Frame for the rest of the course. (2) | 2 | completeness |
| 4 | Your pasted Problem Frame includes every change you describe in Step 3. | 12 | Some of what Step 3 describes shows in the frame, and some does not yet. Point to the line in your frame for each change you name. (9) | The frame is unchanged from First frames while Step 3 says something moved, or it changed with nothing in Step 3 to say why. Your frame and your account of it have to match. (2) | 1 | honesty |
| 5 | In Step 3, you explain what changed in your Problem Frame as a result of your Dojo conversation and what convinced you. If you didn't change anything, you explain why. | 9 | What changed is named, without what convinced you. Say what persuaded you. (6) | "Nothing changed" with no reason, or the Dojo's suggestion restated as the reason. What did you check your frame against? (2) | 3 | honesty |
| 6 | In Step 3, you name one Dojo suggestion you turned down and why, or say you kept them all and why. | 6 | A suggestion is named, turned down or kept, without the reason. The reason is the part that shows your judgment. (4) | Missing. (1) | 4 | judgment |

Total 50. Passing 35. Partial on every line gives 35.

**Grading note.** Line 4 is checked by setting the pasted frame against the learner's First frames submission. Where nothing changed, lines 5 and 6 are scored from the same paragraph: the reason nothing changed usually names the suggestions turned down. Every rung below full names the step and the part. Read for meaning. A blank step is a 1 on its lines.

**What makes it strong.** The change described in Step 3 is visible in Step 2: a line moved, an assumption added, a cost attached. The "why for this course" reason in Step 1 is the same thing Part 6 says you have not seen yet, because that is what Sprint 2 goes and checks. The suggestion turned down shows a judgment the learner made against the AI, in their own words.

**Open, for the first-batch revision.** Whether line 2's example ("the people you can reach or a question you can actually test") steers answers too narrowly. How often "nothing changed" appears, and whether it needs its own rung text.
