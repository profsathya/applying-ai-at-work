# Sprint 1 Self-checks, v0.1

## The shape, from Sathya's rules (27 September)

- About ten items, in the order the learner meets them in the submission. Each is one line: a checkbox, a neutral observable statement of what full points looks like (never "I wrote..."), and its points. Points are set by what the item carries, not divided equally, and add up to the assignment total. The same list serves the learner checking a draft and the grader checking it after.
- Only the full-points line is on the page. Each item also has a 3 rung (partial) and a 1 rung (minimal), a learning-priority rank, and a quality tag, all kept off the page. The rungs reach the learner in the grading comment.
- A checked box earns nothing; the grader verifies every item. Where the learner marks an item done that is not done is what the grader looks at first.
- "What makes it strong" is separate: traceability between parts, so the learner can test a draft against it.

What the renderer offers today: per-task criteria ("What to check in your work", collapsed under each frame) and Melisa's new page-level `final_check` block (PR #167, an open list directly before "Submit your work in Canvas"). Neither carries points or checkboxes; points can be written into the line text. Clickable boxes whose state travels with the Word file would be a renderer feature (Jeremy, item 39).

Rung labels below: 5 = full points, 3 = partial, 1 = minimal. Priority: H, M, L. Tag: completeness, specificity, honesty, judgment.

## First frames, 50 points

Submission: one Word file with three to five frame tables. Items in the order a grader reads a frame, then across frames.

| # | On the page (the 5 line) | Points | 3 rung | 1 rung | Priority | Tag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | At least three frames are present, each with every part filled or marked "I do not know yet." | 6 | Three frames, one or two parts blank without the marker. | Fewer than three frames, or a frame with most parts blank. | H | completeness |
| 2 | Part 2 describes a condition someone is living with, in one or two sentences, not a task, an annoyance, or a solution. | 6 | A condition, but a solution or a "should" leaks in. | A task or a solution stated as the problem. | H | specificity |
| 3 | Part 3a names people or roles, or says who you would ask. | 4 | People named vaguely ("the team") with no role. | Blank, or "everyone." | M | specificity |
| 4 | Part 3b attaches a cost (time, money, mistakes, or stress) to each person or role in 3a. | 6 | A cost for some of the people in 3a. | A cost with nobody attached, or none. | H | specificity |
| 5 | Part 3c is left blank, marked for Sprint 2. | 1 | Filled with a guess. | (same as 3) | L | completeness |
| 6 | Part 4 says what happens now, who does what, and where that stops working well enough, without a fix. | 6 | Current handling described, the shortfall missing or a fix included. | A fix or a "should" in place of the current handling. | H | specificity |
| 7 | Part 5 is one sentence describing a changed situation, not a solution. | 5 | A changed situation with a solution attached. | A solution ("build an app," "hire someone"). | H | judgment |
| 8 | Part 6 lists at least four assumptions, each with the part it comes from, marked confirmed or unverified. | 6 | Four or more assumptions, some unmarked or without a part. | Fewer than four, or none marked. | H | honesty |
| 9 | Every unverified assumption in Part 6 names who could tell you. | 4 | Some unverified lines name nobody. | No unverified line names anyone. | M | honesty |
| 10 | Part 7 lists what is still unknown as questions about the problem, with no tool research or automation ideas. | 3 | Unknowns listed, one is a tool idea. | Blank, or only tool ideas. | M | judgment |
| 11 | Part 1 states a goal nobody involved would dispute and says nothing about what to build. | 3 | A goal that hints at a solution. | A solution, or blank. | M | judgment |

Total: 50.

**What makes it strong.** The cost in 3b is the thing Part 5 would change. The assumptions in Part 6 are the sentences in 2 to 5 that are not from direct observation. The unknowns in Part 7 are the ones the assumptions leave open. Three frames that differ in kind (a process, a habit, a decision) are stronger than three versions of one situation.

**Grading, in the open.** Points per item as above. The Dojo Lab tests these frames next, so the grader's comment on any item at the 3 or 1 rung is the same nudge the Dojo would give: which part, which line, what is missing.

## Problem Frame, 50 points

Submission: three boxes in one Canvas text entry. Items in box order.

| # | On the page (the 5 line) | Points | 3 rung | 1 rung | Priority | Tag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Box 1 answers all four questions in order: the problem and what interests you, why it is workable for nine weeks, why not each of the others, and a runner-up. | 6 | Three of four answered, or out of order. | One or two answered. | H | completeness |
| 2 | "Why not the others" covers every frame tested in the Dojo Lab, including the ones that mostly held up. | 4 | Some frames covered. | Only the chosen one discussed. | M | completeness |
| 3 | "Why for this course" names something in the frame or the testing that makes it workable, not only interest. | 4 | Interest only, or a reason not tied to the frame. | Missing. | M | judgment |
| 4 | A runner-up is named, or the reason none is workable is stated. | 2 | A runner-up with no reason. | Missing. | L | judgment |
| 5 | Box 2 holds all seven parts of the chosen frame, in the learner's words, pasted whole. | 8 | A part missing, or a part in the Dojo's words. | Several parts missing, or the frame summarized rather than pasted. | H | completeness |
| 6 | Part 6 of the pasted frame has every assumption marked confirmed or unverified, each unverified one with who could tell you. | 8 | Some lines unmarked or without a name. | Part 6 unchanged from the first draft with unmarked lines, or empty. | H | honesty |
| 7 | Part 3c of the pasted frame is blank. | 1 | Filled with a guess. | (same as 3) | L | completeness |
| 8 | Box 3 names what moved between the draft and this frame, or states the reason nothing changed. | 6 | A change named without saying where. | "Nothing changed" with no reason. | H | honesty |
| 9 | Box 3 names the argument or evidence that moved it. | 5 | The Dojo's suggestion restated as the evidence. | Missing. | H | judgment |
| 10 | Box 3 names one thing the Dojo suggested that was rejected, and why. | 6 | A rejection with no reason. | Missing. | H | judgment |

Total: 50.

**What makes it strong.** The change in box 3 is visible in box 2: a line moved, an assumption added, a cost attached. The reason in box 1 for "why for this course" is the same thing Part 6 says is unverified, because that is what Sprint 2 will go and check. The rejected suggestion in box 3 shows a judgment the learner made against the AI, in their own words.

**Grading, in the open.** Points per item as above. This frame is what Sprint 2 works on, so a Part 6 at the 3 rung gets the comment "which lines are unverified, and who could tell you," which is the Sprint 2 opening move.
