# Problem Frame

This is your Problem Frame. You wrote its first version in Part D of your Candidate Log at the end of Sprint 1. Copy it here once, in the seven parts below, and from now on this is where it lives. Each sprint asks you to bring it up to date, and each adds a little. Sprint 2 adds a column to part 6 and two short parts at the end. Nothing else you write in the course needs to be carried from sprint to sprint; this document does.

Keep it in your own words. It should read as one document that you could hand to a manager, or use yourself to decide whether to keep going.

Problem:

Last updated (sprint and date):

---

**1. The goal it serves.** One sentence.

\

**2. The problem.** The gap between now and that goal, as a condition someone is living in. One or two sentences.

\

**3a. Who is affected.** The people or specific roles.

\

**3b. What it costs each of them.** In time, money, errors, or strain.

\

**4a. How it is handled today.** The system, process, habit, or person coping with it now, and anything tried and abandoned.

\

**4b. The moment it stops working.** Not the consequence that follows.

\

**5. What fixed would look like.** A changed state, not a solution. One sentence.

\

**6. Your assumptions.** Everything in parts 2 to 5 that you wrote as if it were true without having seen it yourself or heard it from someone who would know. Each one confirmed or unverified, with who could tell you. The last column is added in Sprint 2 and filled in as the course goes: "not yet," or what came back when you checked, in one line, or "accepted, because" and your reason.

*Sprint 2 adds the two parts below.*

**8. What a change would cost.** A few lines. What the problem costs now, from 3b. What any change to how this is handled would ask of whom: time, a habit someone has to change, something that gets worse while it settles, who would have to agree. The time it would take to build anything. Not the cost of a particular solution; the cost of changing at all.

\

**9. Is it worth pursuing?** One paragraph. Worth pursuing, not worth pursuing, or depends on one named thing, with your reasons: what the check said, what exists, what a change would ask. If the answer is no, which runner-up you are taking.
