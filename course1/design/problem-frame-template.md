# Problem Frame

This is your Problem Frame. You wrote its first version in Part D of your Candidate Log at the end of Sprint 1. Copy all of Part D here once, as it stands, and from now on this is where it lives. Each sprint asks you to bring it up to date, and each adds a little.

Every part of it is living. Any of them can change as you find things out, and some will change more than once. That is the point of keeping it in one place.

The whole frame is here from the start. A few parts are marked with the sprint and week that fills them; leave those blank until then. Each sprint's last activity tells you exactly which parts to bring up to date, so you never have to guess.

By the end, you will have one document that outlines your best current understanding of the problem. From it, you can make a call about whether it is a problem worth solving, and start thinking about how you might go about solving it. You can also use the thinking in this frame to make the case to decision-makers or other stakeholders for why to pursue this problem.

**1. The goal it serves.** One sentence.

\

**2. The problem.** The gap between now and that goal, as a condition someone is living in. One or two sentences.

\

**3a. Who is affected.** The people or specific roles.

\

**3b. What the problem costs each of them.** In time, money, errors, or strain.

\

**3c. What fixing this would ask of people.** *Filled in Sprint 2, week 2, from your What already exists table. Leave blank until then.* Not the cost of a particular solution; what any change to how this is handled would ask of the people involved, in 3a or not: a new step, a habit, someone's time, and who would have to agree. Set it beside 3b.

\

**4a. How it is handled today.** The system, process, habit, or person coping with it now, and anything tried and abandoned.

\

**4b. The moment it stops working.** Not the consequence that follows.

\

**5. What fixed would look like.** A changed state, not a solution. One sentence.

\

**6. Your assumptions.** Everything in parts 2 to 5 that you wrote as if it were true without having seen it yourself or heard it from someone who would know.
