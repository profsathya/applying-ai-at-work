Melisa,

While reviewing Sprint 2 we compared the five Introductions. They follow two different templates, and Sprint 5's is a placeholder. This is a proposal for one shape, with what it would mean for your two sprints. Nothing has been changed on any page; it is yours to take or amend.

Sprints 1 and 2 share one shape; Sprints 3 and 4 share another; the section names differ even where the content is the same.

## The proposal

One Introduction shape, in this order, with these names:

1. **Opening.** One paragraph: what the sprint is for and what you will be able to say at the end of it.
2. **Your route through this sprint.** By week, numbered, each item with its kind (page, own your progress, Dojo Lab, graded item, self-check, reflection) and its points. One line each.
3. **Terms**, only where the 31 August rule applies: a term gets a slot if the everyday meaning would mislead, or the sprint runs on it. One or two lines each, and no worked examples here. Otherwise a term is defined on the page where it is first used.
4. **About AI in this sprint.** Which items are own work, which use AI, and where. Two or three sentences; the full version lives in Welcome.
5. **Next up**, naming the first activity.

Two sections Sprints 1 and 2 had, "How to work" and "Where to write," come out. Each activity page says what to open and where to write, and the working reminder (understand the problem, do not checkbox activities; "I do not know" beats making something up) belongs in Welcome with one line on the activity pages where it matters.

Target: 400 to 550 words. Sprint 2 is there now.

## What this means for Sprint 3

The Introduction is about 1,000 words, half of it the Concepts section. Every concept in it is taught again on the page where it is used:

- Stakeholder and the four relationships: on the Stakeholder Map page, one field at a time, with an example and a trap.
- Validation conversation and non-leading questions: on the Stakeholder Map page ("Writing questions that test rather than confirm") and on the Conversation page's Part 1.

One thing has no other home: **Conflicts as information**, with the handover example (the account lead says the real issue is no notice; another manager says there is a tracker nobody updates). The concept check asks about it and the reflection says not to smooth it over, but only the Introduction teaches it. Proposal: move that paragraph, with its example, to the Conversation page's Part 4, where the learner writes up what they heard and is most likely to meet a conflict. The Introduction keeps one line under Terms: a validation conversation tests your frame rather than sells your solution; when stakeholders disagree, the disagreement is the finding.

The route gains weeks and points. The illustration can stay in the opening.

## What this means for Sprint 4

The Introduction is about 780 words with four concepts. Three are taught again where used:

- Gap against topic, with the "until I know this, I can't" test: on Name the Gap and the Dojo Lab.
- Blocking, useful, interesting: on every page.
- Confirmed and inferred: defined on Sprint 3's map page and used throughout Sprint 4.

Two lines have no other home and should move to the Learning Plan page: **closed means answered, not solved**, and **a clear AI explanation is still inferred until you have checked it**. The "Two-Week Sprint Map" folds into the route; "Portfolio Capture" moves to the Learning Plan page, which is where the file lives.

## Two vocabulary notes that touch your pages

- **Risky and high-stakes** name the same test (would the opposite answer change your next move) in Sprint 2 and Sprint 3. Sprint 2 now defines risky as consequence and likelihood together. Proposal: one word across the course, decided by the team; Sprint 2 comes first, so if the word is risky, Sprint 3's "What you are guessing" section and Sprint 4's "Blocking" paragraph change one word each.
- **Sprint 3 is the course's first check.** Sprint 2 no longer asks the learner to check an assumption against a person; its walk rates each assumption's risk and leaves the "not yet" rows for Sprint 3. Sprint 3's page can say so, and its assumption blocks (question to ask, what a different answer changes, high-stakes) can be introduced as the same walk the learner did in Sprint 2, now on guesses about people.

3. A word for the risk test, or a preference the team can rule on.
